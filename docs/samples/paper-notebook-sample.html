Question as printed: Q.1 (a) Three resistors of 4 Ω, 6 Ω and 10 Ω are connected in series across a 20 V supply. Derive the equivalent resistance and find the current.

Derivation — line by line

In series, the same current I flows through every resistor.

V1 = I R1, V2 = I R2, V3 = I R3

Total voltage V = V1 + V2 + V3

V = I (R1 + R2 + R3)

V / I = R1 + R2 + R3

Req = R1 + R2 + R3

Numerical — step by step

  1. GivenR1 = 4 Ω, R2 = 6 Ω, R3 = 10 Ω, V = 20 V
  2. FormulaReq = R1 + R2 + R3
  3. SubstitutionReq = 4 + 6 + 10
  4. CalculateReq = 20 Ω
  5. FormulaI = V / Req
  6. SubstitutionI = 20 / 20
  7. CalculateI = 1 A

Req = 20 Ω, I = 1 A

Check: the equivalent resistance is larger than every individual resistor, which is exactly what series connection must give.

Q.2 (c) An electric heater of resistance 22 Ω works on a 220 V supply for 3 hours. Find the current, the power and the energy consumed in kWh.7 marksQ.2 (c) An electric heater of resistance 22 Ω works on a 220 V supply for 3 hours. Find the current, the power and the energy consumed in kWh.7 ગુણ

Question as printed: Q.2 (c) An electric heater of resistance 22 Ω works on a 220 V supply for 3 hours. Find the current, the power and the energy consumed in kWh.

Given and assumption

R = 22 Ω

V = 220 V

t = 3 h

Assumption: supply voltage stays constant and the heater resistance does not change with temperature.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Formula · currentI = V / R
  2. SubstitutionI = 220 / 22
  3. CalculateI = 10 A
  4. Formula · powerP = V I
  5. SubstitutionP = 220 × 10
  6. CalculateP = 2200 W = 2.2 kW
  7. Formula · energyEnergy = P × t
  8. SubstitutionEnergy = 2.2 kW × 3 h
  9. CalculateEnergy = 6.6 kWh

I = 10 A, P = 2.2 kW, Energy = 6.6 kWh

Interpretation and check

  • 10 A is a normal heater current for a 220 V domestic line, so the order of magnitude is right.
  • 2.2 kW for 3 hours must be about 6.6 units on the electricity meter — matches the calculation.
  • At ₹5.50 per unit the running cost is 6.6 × 5.50 = ₹36.30.

       Category of the Course:             PCC


 Prerequisite:        Students should have basic knowledge of:
                      ●        Fundamentals of computers and operating systems
                      ●        Basic electronics concepts (power supply, voltage, connectors)
                      ●        Computer peripherals and their basic usage
                      ●        Safe handling of electrical equipment
 Rationale:           This  course  provides  essential  knowledge  of  computer  hardware  architecture,
                      including internal components, processor organization, memory hierarchy, storage
                      devices,  and  input/output  peripherals.  It  enables  students  to  understand  system
                      structure, performance factors, and communication between hardware components.
                      The course also emphasizes practical skills in PC assembly, troubleshooting, and
                      preventive maintenance. These competencies are essential for careers in hardware
                      servicing,    technical    support,    system    maintenance,    and    IT    infrastructure
                      management.

Suggested Course Practical List:
    1.  Identify internal components of a desktop computer and observe their functions.
    2.  Study & Comparison of CISC and RISC Architecture
    3.  Identify motherboard components including CPU socket, RAM slots, chipset, expansion slots
        and connectors.
    4.  Observe and list the functions of front panel and back panel ports of a PC.
    5.  Study SMPS connectors and measure output voltages using a digital multimeter.
    6.  Install and remove RAM modules and verify detection in BIOS/UEFI.
    7.  Install HDD/SSD and connect SATA/NVMe interface cables.
    8.  Configure BIOS/UEFI settings and observe hardware detection parameters.
    9.  Identify different types of memory modules and storage devices.
    10. Connect and test input devices such as keyboard, mouse and scanner.
    11. Install and configure a printer and perform a test print.
    12. Identify monitor ports and configure display settings in the operating system.
    13. Perform POST and interpret beep codes and startup error messages.
    14. Troubleshoot common hardware faults such as no display, boot failure, and RAM errors.
    15. Assemble and disassemble a PC cabinet following safety and ESD precautions.

List of Laboratory/Learning Resources Required:
    1.  Desktop computers with basic configuration and Windows/Linux operating systems.
    2.  Computer hardware components for practice (motherboard, CPU, RAM, SMPS, HDD/SSD).
    3.  PC hardware tool kit (screwdriver set, anti-static wrist strap, cleaning brush, thermal paste).
    4.  Digital multimeter for power supply testing and diagnostics.
    5.  Diagnostic and utility software (CPU-Z, HWMonitor, MemTest86, disk diagnostic tools).
    6.  Input devices such as keyboard, mouse, and scanner.
    7.  Output devices such as LED/LCD monitors and printers.
    8.  Storage devices, including pen drives, external HDD/SSD, and optical media.
    9.  Network cables, connectors, and internet connectivity.
    10. UPS or a power backup system for safe operation.

Suggested Activities for Students:
Other than classroom and laboratory learning, the following  co-curricular activities are suggested to
accelerate attainment of course outcomes. Students should perform these activities in groups and prepare
brief reports with supporting evidence for their academic portfolio and placement readiness.
             a)  Conduct group discussions on types of memory and memory hierarchy.
             b)  Undertakes   micro-projects   related   to   PC   assembly,   troubleshooting,   or   hardware
                 comparison.
             c)  Deliver seminars on the latest processor architectures (e.g., Intel Core series, AMD
                 Ryzen).
             d)  Prepare charts and comparison posters on hardware components such as HDD vs SSD,
                 RAM types, or motherboard form factors.
             e)  Collect and study older  storage devices (floppy  disks, magnetic tapes, CDs/DVDs) to
                 understand
                 technological evolution.
             f)  Demonstrate PC assembly and disassembly procedures.
             g)  Prepare preventive maintenance checklists for computer labs.
             h)  Visit a computer service center or IT lab to observe hardware maintenance practices.

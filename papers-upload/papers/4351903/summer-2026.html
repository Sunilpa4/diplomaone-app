Question: Q1(a) State the advantages of the MPFI system.

  • One injector at each intake port supplies fuel close to its cylinder; cylinder-to-cylinder mixture distribution is more uniform than a common carburettor.
  • ECU meters fuel using sensor inputs and engine conditions, improving starting, throttle response and mixture control.
  • Can improve fuel economy, power and exhaust emissions when correctly calibrated.
  • Fewer carburettor-related problems such as icing and mixture maldistribution; altitude/temperature compensation is easier.
  • Limitations to note: higher cost/complexity, need for electrical sensors/ECU and specialist diagnosis.

પ્રશ્ન: Q1(a) MPFI systemના ફાયદા જણાવો.

  • દરેક intake port પર injector હોવાથી fuel cylinderની નજીક જાય; common carburettor કરતાં દરેક cylinderમાં mixture વધુ સમાન.
  • ECU sensor અને engine conditionથી fuel માપે; starting, throttle response અને mixture control સુધરે.
  • સાચું calibration હોય તો fuel economy, power અને exhaust emission સુધરી શકે.
  • Carburettor icing અને mixture અસમાનતા જેવી સમસ્યા ઓછી; altitude/temperature મુજબ compensation સરળ.

Question: Q1(b) Explain battery coil ignition system with line diagram.

Purpose: produce a correctly timed high-voltage spark at each petrol-engine spark plug.

Main parts: battery, ignition switch, ignition coil (primary and secondary windings), ballast resistor if fitted, contact breaker or electronic switching unit, condenser/capacitor (traditional points system), distributor and spark plugs.

  1. With ignition on and breaker/driver closed, low-voltage current flows in the coil primary and stores magnetic energy.
  2. At the correct crank angle, the breaker opens or the ECU switches the primary off quickly.
  3. The collapsing magnetic field induces a high voltage in the secondary winding.
  4. The distributor routes the high-tension pulse to the correct cylinder plug (or coil-on-plug supplies each cylinder directly); the spark ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture.
  5. The condenser across traditional contact points reduces arcing and helps the field collapse rapidly.

Draw the low-voltage primary circuit and high-voltage secondary/distributor circuit separately; label battery, coil, switch, breaker/ECU, distributor and plugs.

પ્રશ્ન: Q1(b) Line diagram સાથે battery coil ignition system સમજાવો.

હેતુ: petrol engineના દરેક spark plug પર યોગ્ય સમયે ઊંચા voltageની spark બનાવવી.

મુખ્ય ભાગો: battery, ignition switch, primary/secondary windingવાળી ignition coil, હોય તો ballast resistor, contact breaker અથવા electronic switch, જૂની points systemમાં condenser/capacitor, distributor અને spark plug.

  1. Ignition ચાલુ અને breaker/driver બંધ હોય ત્યારે coil primaryમાં low-voltage current વહે અને magnetic energy સંગ્રહાય.
  2. યોગ્ય crank angleએ breaker ખૂલે અથવા ECU primary current ઝડપથી બંધ કરે.
  3. તૂટતા magnetic fieldથી secondary windingમાં ઊંચું voltage પ્રેરાય.
  4. Distributor high-tension pulse યોગ્ય cylinder plug સુધી મોકલે (coil-on-plugમાં દરેક cylinderને સીધી coil); spark compressed air-fuel mixture સળગાવે.
  5. જૂના contact points પરનો condenser arcing ઘટાડે અને field ઝડપથી તૂટવામાં મદદ કરે.

Question: Q1(c) OR · Determine Indicated power, Break power and Mechanical efficiency if following observation for the four stroke one cylinder compression ignition engine are made. (1) Cylinder bore diameter =0.1 m (2) Stroke length = 0.15 m (3) Mean effective pressure =7.5 bar (4) Speed =450 r.p.m. (5) Break wheel diameter =0.6 m (6) Brake load =220 N (7) Observation of spring gauge =20 N.

Given: D=0.10 m; L=0.15 m; pm=7.5 bar=750,000 Pa; N=450 rpm; brake-wheel diameter=0.60 m; applied load W=220 N; spring reading S=20 N. Assume wheel diameter is effective brake diameter and rope thickness is neglected (not supplied).

Step 1 — piston area and swept volume:

A=πD²/4=π(0.10)²/4=0.007854 m²
Vs=AL=0.007854×0.15=0.0011781 m³/rev

Step 2 — indicated power: a four-stroke cylinder has one working cycle every two revolutions.

IP=pm×Vs×(N/2)/60
=750,000×0.0011781×(450/2)/60
=3313.4 W = 3.313 kW

Step 3 — brake torque: net rope force is the load difference, not the sum.

F=W−S=220−20=200 N
r=Dw/2=0.60/2=0.30 m
T=Fr=200×0.30=60 N·m

Step 4 — brake power:

BP=2πNT/60=2π×450×60/60=2827.4 W=2.827 kW

Step 5 — mechanical efficiency:

ηm=BP/IP×100=2.827/3.313×100=85.3%

IP=3.313 kW; BP=2.827 kW; mechanical efficiency=85.3%.

Question: Q2 Set A(b) Explain fuel supply system of petrol engine with block diagram.

Conventional carburetted system — flow: fuel tank → strainer/pick-up → fuel pump → filter → carburettor float chamber/jet → air-fuel mixture through intake manifold → cylinder. The pump supplies fuel; the float/needle maintains a near-constant chamber level; venturi depression draws and meters fuel through the jet; throttle controls mixture flow/power.

Working:

  1. Fuel is stored safely in the tank and drawn through a strainer.
  2. A mechanical/electric pump delivers it through a filter to the carburettor.
  3. In the venturi, air velocity rises and pressure falls; fuel is drawn from the jet and atomised/mixed with air.
  4. The throttle/butterfly controls mixture entering the intake manifold; manifold distributes it to cylinders.
  5. Choke/enrichment assists cold starting; modern engines commonly replace the carburettor with ECU-controlled port injection.

Label tank, pump, filter, carburettor, air cleaner, throttle, manifold and engine. The block diagram is the accepted line diagram for a system question.

Question: Q2 Set A(c) Explain sequential CNG system.

Meaning: a sequential CNG kit meters gas separately to each cylinder in firing order, coordinated by an electronic control unit (ECU). It is not simply a common mixer feeding all cylinders.

  1. High-pressure CNG is stored in an approved cylinder with manual/electrical shut-off and excess-flow/safety devices.
  2. Gas passes through a high-pressure line and filter to a regulator/reducer, which reduces cylinder pressure in controlled stages and compensates for engine/manifold conditions.
  3. Low-pressure gas reaches a filter and injector rail; one injector/nozzle is assigned to each cylinder/intake runner.
  4. ECU reads engine speed/load and relevant sensors and opens injectors sequentially in synchronism with each cylinder's intake event.
  5. Petrol/CNG changeover, start strategy, temperature/pressure compensation and diagnostics are controlled electronically.
  6. Leak checks, ventilation, approved components, pressure relief and shut-off interlocks are essential.

Advantages: accurate cylinder-wise metering, smoother operation and improved emissions/economy versus a simple mixer when correctly calibrated. Limitations: cost, calibration, cylinder/tank space and reduced range compared with liquid fuel by volume.

પ્રશ્ન: Q2 Set A(c) Sequential CNG system સમજાવો.

અર્થ: Sequential CNG kit ECUના નિયંત્રણથી firing order પ્રમાણે દરેક cylinderને અલગ gas માપીને આપે. બધા cylinderને એક common mixer આપવું જ sequential system નથી.

  1. High-pressure CNG approved cylinderમાં manual/electrical shut-off અને safety device સાથે સંગ્રહાય.
  2. High-pressure line/filter પછી regulator/reducer cylinder pressureને નિયંત્રિત તબક્કામાં ઘટાડે અને engine/manifold condition મુજબ compensate કરે.
  3. Low-pressure gas filter અને injector rail સુધી જાય; દરેક cylinder/intake runner માટે injector/nozzle હોય.
  4. ECU engine speed/load અને sensor વાંચી દરેક cylinderના intake event સાથે મેળ ખાતા ક્રમે injector ખોલે.
  5. Petrol/CNG changeover, starting, temperature/pressure compensation અને diagnostics electronic રીતે નિયંત્રાય.
  6. Leak check, ventilation, approved component, pressure relief અને shut-off interlock જરૂરી.

લાભ: cylinder-wise ચોક્કસ metering, સરળ ચાલ અને સારા calibrationમાં mixer કરતાં emission/economy સુધરે. મર્યાદા: ખર્ચ, calibration, જગ્યા અને liquid fuel કરતાં volume દીઠ range ઓછી.

  1. Propane is C3H8; butane is C4H10. Both are saturated hydrocarbons and principal LPG components.

Question: Q3 Set A(a) List various methods of improving performance of gas turbine and explain any one.

Methods: raise turbine-inlet temperature within material/cooling limits; select a suitable pressure ratio; improve compressor/turbine isentropic efficiency; use regeneration/recuperation; intercool compression; reheat expansion; reduce inlet/exhaust pressure losses; or combine the gas turbine with a steam bottoming cycle.

Regeneration: turbine exhaust is often hotter than compressor-discharge air at moderate pressure ratios. A recuperator transfers exhaust heat to compressed air before the combustor. The combustor then needs less fuel to reach the same turbine-inlet temperature, so thermal efficiency improves. It is beneficial only when exhaust temperature exceeds compressor-outlet temperature and the heat exchanger's pressure losses/cost do not outweigh the gain.

Intercooling primarily reduces compressor work; reheating primarily raises turbine work; regeneration primarily saves fuel/raises efficiency. Do not claim every method always increases both efficiency and net output.

Question: Q3 Set A(b) Explain various process of Brayton cycle with the help of P -V and T-S diagram.

The ideal air-standard Brayton (Joule) cycle has two isentropic and two constant-pressure processes:

  1. 1–2, isentropic compression: compressor raises pressure and temperature; specific volume falls. On T–s it is vertical upward; on P–v it follows the compression curve.
  2. 2–3, constant-pressure heat addition: combustor/heater adds heat; temperature and specific volume increase at high pressure.
  3. 3–4, isentropic expansion: turbine produces work; pressure and temperature fall and specific volume increases. On T–s it is vertical downward.
  4. 4–1, constant-pressure heat rejection: the ideal closed cycle rejects heat at low pressure and returns to state 1.

Draw and label both P–v and T–s plots with states 1–4 and arrows. In a real open gas turbine, intake/exhaust replace the literal closed heat-rejection loop of the air-standard model.

Question: Q4 Set A(a) Define COP of refrigerator and heat pump with formulas.

Coefficient of performance (COP) is desired heat-transfer effect divided by work input; it is dimensionless.

  • Refrigerator: desired effect is heat removed from the low-temperature space, QL.
    COPR=QL/Win
  • Heat pump: desired effect is heat delivered to the warm space, QH.
    COPHP=QH/Win

Energy balance: QH=QL+Win, so for the same cycle COPHP=COPR+1. COP is not an efficiency percentage and can exceed 1.

Question: Q4 Set B(b) State the difficulties encountered in the reverse Carnot cycle for vapor form medium.

  • Isentropic compression from saturated vapour commonly enters the superheated region, raising discharge temperature and requiring a large compression ratio/heat rejection management.
  • The ideal reverse-Carnot cycle can require compression of a wet vapour mixture if states are chosen on the saturation dome; liquid droplets can damage a practical compressor and dilute oil.
  • Isentropic expansion through a turbine produces a two-phase mixture; a small refrigeration system's wet expansion turbine is costly, hard to control and may suffer blade erosion/leakage.
  • Expansion work recovered is often small compared with turbine cost/complexity; a practical throttling valve is simpler though it loses available work.
  • Isothermal heat transfer at finite temperature differences would require very large heat-transfer surfaces or very slow operation; real processes are not perfectly reversible.

Therefore practical vapour-compression cycles use a compressor handling dry/superheated vapour and an isenthalpic throttle rather than a wet compressor and expansion turbine.

Question: Q4 Set B(c) Explain the vapor absorption system with block diagram.

Principle: an absorption system replaces most mechanical compressor work with heat supplied to the generator. It uses a refrigerant-absorbent pair and a solution pump.

Main components: evaporator, absorber, solution pump, solution heat exchanger, generator, condenser, refrigerant expansion valve and solution pressure-reducing valve; cooling-water/heat-source connections are also needed.

  1. Low-pressure refrigerant evaporates in the evaporator and absorbs heat from the refrigerated space.
  2. Refrigerant vapour enters the absorber and is absorbed by the absorbent; heat of absorption is rejected. The resulting strong solution is pumped to high pressure.
  3. The solution heat exchanger preheats the strong solution before the generator.
  4. Generator heat drives refrigerant vapour out of the solution. The weak solution returns through the heat exchanger and pressure-reducing valve to the absorber.
  5. High-pressure refrigerant vapour condenses in the condenser, rejects heat, then throttles to evaporator pressure and repeats the cycle.

Common pairs: NH₃–H₂O (ammonia refrigerant; water absorbent) and H₂O–LiBr (water refrigerant; lithium-bromide absorbent; used above freezing). The pump work is small relative to compressor work, but generator heat and cooling are required.

Question: Q5 Set A(b) Explain superheating of suction vapor in the refrigeration system with the help of P-h and T-S diagram and state its effect.

Definition: suction-vapour superheating is heating refrigerant vapour above its saturation temperature at evaporator/suction pressure before it enters the compressor.

  • On P-h, the low-pressure evaporator outlet moves right from saturated-vapour state 1 to superheated state 1′ at nearly constant pressure; enthalpy increases.
  • On T-s, the state moves into the superheated region at low pressure.
  • Useful superheat inside the evaporator: may absorb additional heat from the refrigerated space and ensures dry vapour reaches the compressor.
  • Parasitic superheat in an uninsulated suction line outside the cooled space: adds heat not useful to the load, raises compressor inlet specific volume and discharge temperature; for a fixed-displacement compressor it can reduce refrigerant mass flow/capacity.
  • Compressor work per kg generally increases; COP can increase or decrease depending on refrigerant, cycle and where superheat is obtained. It helps prevent liquid slugging, but excessive superheat is undesirable.

Mark the evaporator outlet 1, superheated suction state 1′, low-pressure line and direction on both diagrams.

Question: Q5 Set B(c) Determine (1) vapor pressure (2) specific humidity and (3) specific enthalpy of moisture saturated air at 35°C.

Assumptions/data: saturated moist air at T=35°C; total pressure not stated, so assume standard atmospheric pressure P=101.325 kPa. From saturated-water tables at 35°C, water-vapour saturation pressure pws≈5.628 kPa. Use Ra/Rv≈0.622, cpa=1.005 and cpv=1.88 kJ/(kg·K).

  1. Vapour pressure: saturation means pv=pws=5.628 kPa.
  2. Humidity ratio (usual psychrometric “specific humidity” basis, kg vapour/kg dry air):
    ω=0.622 pv/(P−pv)
    =0.622×5.628/(101.325−5.628)=0.03658 kg/kg dry air
  3. If specific humidity is defined per kg of moist air:
    q=ω/(1+ω)=0.03658/1.03658=0.03529 kg vapour/kg moist air
  4. Specific enthalpy of moist air per kg dry air:
    h=1.005T+ω(2500+1.88T)
    =1.005×35+0.03658(2500+1.88×35)
    =35.175+93.86≈129.0 kJ/kg dry air

pv≈5.628 kPa; ω≈0.0366 kg/kg dry air (q≈0.0353 kg/kg moist air); h≈129.0 kJ/kg dry air.

Check/notes: the pressure assumption is necessary because humidity ratio depends on total pressure. Some tables use slightly different h reference constants, so enthalpy may vary by a few tenths of a kJ/kg.

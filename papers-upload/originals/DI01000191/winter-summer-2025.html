Q1(a)Define: (i) Force (ii) Energy (iii) Industry 4.0.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Force is a push or pull (an interaction) that can change an object's motion or deform it. It is a vector; for constant mass, the resultant force is F = ma. SI unit: newton (N).
  • Energy is a conserved physical quantity associated with the ability to cause change or do work. Common forms include kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical and electrical energy. SI unit: joule (J).
  • Industry 4.0 is the integration of connected sensors, cyber-physical systems, data networks, automation and analytics in manufacturing. It enables real-time monitoring, traceability and predictive maintenance; people still supervise decisions and safety.

Q1(c)Explain the functioning of the following boiler mountings: (i) Feed check valve (ii) Safety valve (iii) Fusible plug (iv) Pressure gauge.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

  1. Feed check valve: fitted in the feedwater line where water enters the boiler. It regulates feed admission and acts as a non-return valve, opposing reverse flow of hot boiler water into the feed line when feed-pump pressure falls. Its design must suit the boiler pressure and service.
  2. Safety valve: an automatic pressure-protection device set to lift at its certified pressure. If boiler pressure rises beyond the permitted limit, the valve opens and discharges steam; when pressure falls to the reseating range it closes. It must never be adjusted or obstructed by an unauthorised person.
  3. Fusible plug: a protective plug fitted at a specified position over the furnace. Water normally keeps its fusible core cooled. If the water level falls far enough to uncover it, excessive heat melts the core and opens a passage, warning of dangerous low-water overheating and helping limit furnace damage. It is not a substitute for water-level indication or other protection.
  4. Pressure gauge: indicates the steam pressure in the boiler, commonly through a pressure-responsive element such as a Bourdon tube moving a pointer over a calibrated scale. The reading lets an authorised operator monitor pressure; the instrument is not itself a pressure-control or relief device.

These are safety-critical boiler fittings; the answer describes their purpose, not an operating or testing procedure.

Q1(c)Explain the working of the following boilers: (i) Cochran boiler (ii) Babcock and Wilcox boiler.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

(i) Cochran boiler

  • Type: a traditional vertical, multi-tubular, internally fired fire-tube boiler.
  • Main parts: vertical shell and steam space, furnace and grate, combustion chamber, fire tubes, smoke box and flue/chimney path. Water surrounds the furnace and fire-tube heating surfaces.
  • Working: fuel burns on the grate. Hot gases pass from the furnace through the combustion chamber and fire tubes to the smoke box and flue. Heat crosses the tube walls to the surrounding water. The heated water circulates, steam separates and collects in the upper steam space, and steam is taken from the outlet.

(ii) Babcock and Wilcox boiler

  • Type: a traditional externally fired water-tube boiler.
  • Main parts: upper steam-and-water drum, inclined water tubes connected to headers, furnace, baffles and gas/flue path. Water is inside the tubes; hot gases pass around them.
  • Working: furnace gases are directed by baffles across the tube bank. Water heated in the tubes rises through the circulation circuit; cooler, denser water returns through the designed path. Steam separates in the drum and is drawn off. The tube bank provides the heat-transfer surface.

The key distinction is fire-tube Cochran versus water-tube Babcock and Wilcox. Actual pressure, capacity and arrangement depend on the certified design.

Q2(a)Define: (i) Boyle's law (ii) Combined gas law.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Boyle's law: for a fixed mass of gas at constant absolute temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume: pV = constant;   p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
  • Combined gas law: for the same fixed mass of ideal gas between two equilibrium states, p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂. Use absolute pressure and absolute temperature (kelvin); mass and gas composition must remain unchanged.
Q2(b)Explain the functioning of the following boiler accessories: (i) Superheater (ii) Economizer.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

  1. Superheater: carries saturated steam through a bank of tubes exposed to hot flue gases. Heat raises the steam temperature above the saturation temperature at the same pressure, producing superheated steam. This increases steam energy and reduces condensation in suitable downstream equipment.
  2. Economizer: passes boiler feedwater through a heat exchanger in the flue-gas path. Waste heat from the outgoing gases preheats the feedwater before it enters the boiler, improving heat recovery and reducing the fuel required for a given steam output.

A superheater heats steam; an economizer heats feedwater. Neither performs the safety-valve function.

Q2(a)Define: (i) Charles's law (ii) Universal gas constant.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

The source prints a duplicated “(ii)” before the second item; the intended two-part numbering is shown here.

  • Charles's law: for a fixed mass of gas at constant absolute pressure, its volume is directly proportional to its absolute temperature: V/T = constant;   V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂. Temperature must be in kelvin.
  • Universal gas constant, Ru: the common molar gas constant in the ideal-gas equation pV = nRuT. Its value is approximately 8.314 J/(mol·K), equivalently 8.314 kJ/(kmol·K). The mass-specific constant is different for each gas: R = Ru/M.
Q2(b)Mention the different types of boilers.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Boilers can be classified on several independent bases:

  • By fluid in tubes: fire-tube (hot gases inside tubes, water outside) and water-tube (water/steam inside tubes, hot gases outside).
  • By axis: vertical, horizontal or inclined.
  • By firing: internally fired or externally fired.
  • By circulation: natural-circulation or forced-circulation.
  • By service/use: stationary, locomotive, marine or process boiler.

These categories can overlap: for example, a boiler may be horizontal, water-tube and stationary. Cochran is a common fire-tube example; Babcock and Wilcox is a common water-tube example.

Q2(c)Explain the working of a four-stroke diesel engine with a diagram.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A conventional diesel is a compression-ignition (CI) internal-combustion engine. The cycle requires four piston strokes, or two crankshaft revolutions, with one principal power stroke per cylinder.

  1. Intake (TDC to BDC): the inlet valve is open and the piston descends, drawing air into the cylinder; the exhaust valve is closed.
  2. Compression (BDC to TDC): both valves are nominally closed and the piston compresses the air. Its pressure and temperature rise. Near the end of the stroke, fuel is injected into the hot air and self-ignites.
  3. Power / expansion (TDC to BDC): combustion raises gas pressure; expanding gases push the piston down. The connecting rod turns the crankshaft; both valves are nominally closed during the main power period.
  4. Exhaust (BDC to TDC): the exhaust valve opens and the rising piston expels burnt gases; the inlet valve is closed in the simplified diagram.

Actual valve timing includes advance, delay and overlap, which are omitted from a basic cycle sketch.

Q3(a)Explain the working of a vapour-compression refrigeration system.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

A vapour-compression refrigeration system circulates refrigerant through four main components:

  1. Evaporator: low-pressure refrigerant absorbs heat from the cooled space and evaporates.
  2. Compressor: draws in vapour and uses mechanical work to raise its pressure and temperature.
  3. Condenser: rejects heat to the surroundings; the high-pressure vapour condenses to liquid.
  4. Expansion device: throttles the liquid to a lower pressure; part flashes to vapour and the cold mixture returns to the evaporator.

Thus heat is taken from the refrigerated space and rejected outside, using compressor work.

Q3(b)A closed cylinder of volume 1.8 m³ is filled with oxygen at 50 bar. If the temperature is 26 °C and the molecular weight of oxygen is 32 kg/kmol, find the mass of oxygen.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Assumption: treat the stated 50 bar as absolute pressure, since the paper does not say gauge or absolute. Model oxygen as an ideal gas.

Given: p = 50 bar = 5000 kPa; V = 1.8 m³; T = 26 + 273.15 = 299.15 K; M = 32 kg/kmol; Ru = 8.314 kJ/(kmol·K).

Specific gas constant: R = Ru/M = 8.314/32 = 0.2598 kJ/(kg·K).

pV = mRT   ⇒   m = pV/(RT)

m = (5000 kPa × 1.8 m³) / (0.2598 kJ/(kg·K) × 299.15 K) = 115.8 kg (approximately).

Unit check: kPa·m³ = kJ, so the result is in kg. If 50 bar was intended as gauge pressure, first add local atmospheric pressure to obtain absolute pressure; the mass would then be slightly higher.

Q3(c)Explain the construction and working of a reciprocating pump.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A reciprocating pump is a positive-displacement pump. The following describes a conventional single-acting arrangement.

Construction

A cylinder contains a close-fitting piston connected by a rod to a crank and connecting rod. A suction pipe with a non-return suction valve connects the cylinder to the liquid source; a delivery pipe with a non-return delivery valve leads to the outlet. The valves open and close automatically in response to pressure difference.

Working

  1. Suction stroke: the crank moves the piston away from the cylinder head, increasing cylinder volume and lowering its pressure. The suction valve opens; the delivery valve stays closed; liquid enters the cylinder.
  2. Delivery stroke: the piston moves back, reducing volume and raising pressure. The suction valve closes; once cylinder pressure exceeds delivery pressure, the delivery valve opens and liquid is forced into the delivery pipe.

One delivery event occurs per cycle in a single-acting pump, so discharge is pulsating. An air vessel may smooth flow in an appropriately designed installation.

Q3(c)Explain the construction and working of a single-stage reciprocating compressor.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A single-stage reciprocating compressor compresses air from inlet to delivery pressure in one compression stage.

Construction

The main parts are a cylinder and head, piston with rings, piston rod, connecting rod and crankshaft, suction (inlet) and delivery valves, drive, lubrication and cooling arrangements. A filter, receiver, pressure gauge, control and certified pressure-relief protection may form part of the overall system. The receiver stores compressed air; it is not the compressor cylinder.

Working

  1. Suction: piston motion increases cylinder volume. After residual clearance air expands and cylinder pressure falls below inlet pressure, the suction valve opens and air enters; the delivery valve remains closed.
  2. Compression: the return motion reduces volume, the suction valve closes and trapped air is compressed; its temperature generally rises.
  3. Delivery: when cylinder pressure exceeds receiver/delivery pressure sufficiently, the delivery valve opens and compressed air leaves. A small clearance volume remains at the end of travel.

Compressed air stores hazardous energy; the sketch is conceptual, not a service or pressure-vessel instruction.

Q4(a)Explain the different types of material-handling equipment.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Material-handling equipment is commonly grouped by the way it moves or supports a load. Categories overlap, but four useful groups are:

  • Hoisting equipment: raises or lowers suspended loads; examples include a chain block, hoist and crane.
  • Conveying equipment: moves material along a planned path; examples include belt, roller, screw and bucket conveyors.
  • Surface equipment: carries loads on the floor; examples include trolleys, hand trucks, pallet trucks and forklifts.
  • Overhead equipment: travels on an elevated support and saves floor space; examples include bridge cranes and monorails.

Choose the actual device for the load, route, capacity, throughput and safety conditions.

Q4(b)Explain the different types of air compressor.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Air compressors are primarily classified by how they raise pressure:

ClassTypes and principle
Positive displacementReciprocating piston: traps air in a cylinder and reduces its volume. Rotary screw: traps air between rotating screw elements and progressively reduces the space. Rotary vane: sliding vanes form changing-volume chambers.
Dynamic (rotodynamic)Centrifugal: an impeller adds velocity to air and a diffuser converts part of that velocity to pressure. Axial-flow: rotating blade rows impart energy as air flows mainly along the shaft axis.

Compressors may also be described as single- or multi-stage, and reciprocating units as single- or double-acting. These are separate classification bases.

Q4(c)Explain the working of universal and Oldham couplings.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Universal coupling (Hooke's joint)

Two yokes fixed to the shafts are connected by a cross or spider with mutually perpendicular trunnions. The joint transmits torque while allowing the shaft axes to meet at a designed angle. A single conventional Hooke joint at an angle does not give uniform output angular speed: its output speed fluctuates during a revolution. Correctly arranged double or constant-velocity joints can address this under their design conditions.

Oldham coupling

Each shaft carries a hub with a slot. An intermediate disc has tongues on opposite faces at right angles; each tongue slides in one hub slot. This sliding action transmits rotation while allowing a limited parallel offset between shaft axes. It is not a substitute for a universal joint's angular connection.

Both couplings have finite alignment, speed and torque limits; installation and guarding must follow the design.

Q4(a)Explain the working principle of an air compressor.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

An air compressor takes in air at a lower pressure, receives mechanical work from a driver and delivers air at a higher pressure. In a positive-displacement compressor, air is trapped and its available volume is reduced (as in a piston cylinder). In a dynamic compressor, a rotating element adds velocity and a diffuser converts some of that velocity into pressure. Compression generally raises air temperature; cooling may be needed in a practical system.

Q4(b)Explain the working principle of material-handling equipment.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

The basic principle is to move, position or store a material by applying a controlled force along a planned route while supporting the load safely. The mechanism depends on the task:

  • A hoist uses a rated chain/rope, drum or mechanical arrangement to lift or lower a suspended load.
  • A conveyor moves material continuously or stepwise along a fixed path using a belt, rollers, screw or buckets.
  • Surface equipment supports a load on wheels or forks for floor-level transport.
  • An overhead crane combines hoisting with trolley/bridge travel across a designed structure.

Safe selection checks load mass and shape, route, lift height, capacity at the actual configuration, people interaction and guarding. Mechanical advantage trades force for distance; it does not create energy.

Q4(c)Explain the working of: (i) Disc clutch (ii) Shoe brake.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

(i) Disc clutch

A typical single-plate clutch has a flywheel on the driving shaft, a friction-lined clutch plate splined to the driven shaft, a pressure plate and springs, plus a release bearing/fork. Engaged: springs clamp the clutch plate between flywheel and pressure plate; friction transmits torque to the driven shaft. Disengaged: operating the release mechanism unloads the clamp so the surfaces separate and drive is interrupted. Slip during engagement produces heat, so the clutch must be correctly rated.

(ii) Shoe brake

A shoe (or block) brake presses a friction-lined shoe against a rotating drum or wheel. A lever, cam or other designed actuator applies the normal force; friction acts tangentially opposite motion and creates a braking torque, converting mechanical energy mainly into heat. Releasing the actuator removes contact (often with a return spring). Internal-expanding and external-shoe layouts differ, but both require suitable lining, force, heat capacity and safe rating.

A clutch connects a driving and driven member; a brake resists or stops motion. Neither diagram alone establishes a safe holding or stopping capacity.

Main question set — answer (a), (b) and (c)
Q5(a)Explain the various types of steam.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

At a specified pressure, the main steam conditions are:

  • Wet steam: a saturated mixture of liquid-water droplets and water vapour; its dryness fraction (quality) is between 0 and 1.
  • Dry saturated steam: saturated vapour at the vapour boundary with no liquid fraction in the ideal state; its quality is 1 by the endpoint convention.
  • Superheated steam: single-phase vapour heated above the saturation temperature at the same pressure. Saturated-mixture quality is not assigned to a superheated state.
Q5(b)Explain the basic concept of: (i) Priming (ii) Industry 4.0.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

(i) Boiler priming

Priming is the carryover of liquid boiler water with the outgoing steam. It increases steam moisture and may carry dissolved impurities into downstream equipment. High water level, sudden load changes, excessive steam velocity and foaming can encourage carryover. Suitable water-level control, water chemistry, load management and steam separation help control it. This is different from initially filling a pump with liquid, which is also called priming.

(ii) Industry 4.0

Industry 4.0 links machines, sensors, control systems and networks so production data can be monitored and analysed. Connected equipment and analytics can support automation, traceability, condition monitoring and predictive-maintenance alerts. Reliable data, cybersecurity and human verification remain essential.

Q5(c)Describe the various types of gears used in power transmission.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Gears transmit motion by meshing teeth. Common types are:

  • Spur gears: straight teeth, normally for parallel shafts; simple and efficient but tooth engagement can be noisier at high speed.
  • Helical gears: teeth are inclined to the shaft axis; progressive engagement gives smoother, quieter running. The helix also creates axial thrust that must be supported.
  • Bevel gears: conical gears used mainly for intersecting shafts, often at right angles.
  • Worm and worm wheel: screw-like worm meshes with a wheel, commonly connecting non-parallel, non-intersecting shafts and providing a large reduction in one stage. Self-locking must not be assumed.
  • Rack and pinion: a circular pinion meshes with a straight rack to convert rotary motion to linear motion.

Gear choice depends on shaft arrangement, ratio, load, speed, noise, lubrication and housing. A simple external gear pair reverses rotation; its ideal ratio follows tooth counts.

Q5(a)Explain the basic concept of a shaft and an axle.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Shaft: a rotating machine member that commonly transmits torque/power and supports components such as gears or pulleys. It is designed for torsion and often bending as well.
  • Axle: a member whose main function is to support a rotating wheel or other component and carry its load; it may be stationary or may rotate, depending on the design. An idler-wheel axle chiefly supports load, while vehicle terminology sometimes includes torque-transmitting drive axles.

Thus, “shaft transmits torque” and “axle supports rotating parts” are useful introductory distinctions, not universal rules for every vehicle design.

Q5(b)Explain the basic concept of: (i) Dryness fraction (ii) Degree of superheat.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

  • Dryness fraction, x (steam quality): in a saturated wet-steam mixture it is the mass of vapour divided by the total mass of liquid plus vapour: x = mv/(mv + ml). It is dimensionless. For a wet mixture 0 < x < 1; dry saturated steam has x = 1. It is a mass fraction, not a volume fraction.
  • Degree of superheat: the temperature excess of superheated steam over the saturation temperature at the same pressure: degree of superheat = Tsup − Tsat(p). It is a temperature difference, expressed in K or °C; it is not heat energy.

Q1(c)Define: wet steam, dry steam, superheated steam and dryness fraction of steam.7 marks⌄

Q1(c)Define: sensible heat, latent heat, wetness fraction and degree of superheat.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

  • Sensible heat: heat transfer that changes a substance's temperature without a phase change under the stated process conditions. For a simple constant-specific-heat estimate, Q = mcΔT.
  • Latent heat: energy transferred per unit mass during a phase change at the saturation condition; for a pure substance at constant pressure, temperature remains at saturation while the phase changes. Specific latent heat is in kJ/kg.
  • Wetness fraction, y: in a wet saturated-steam mixture, the liquid-water mass divided by total mixture mass: y = ml/(mv + ml) = 1 − x. It is dimensionless.
  • Degree of superheat: actual superheated-steam temperature minus the saturation temperature at the same pressure: Tsup − Tsat(p). It is a temperature difference, not an amount of heat.

Q2(b)Describe the combined gas law.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

For a fixed mass and composition of ideal gas between two equilibrium states, the combined gas law relates pressure, volume and absolute temperature:

p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂   (or pV/T = constant).

It follows by combining the special gas-law relations. If temperature is constant it reduces to Boyle's law; if pressure is constant it reduces to Charles's law. Use absolute pressure, temperature in kelvin, and consistent units. The mass/composition must not change between states.

Q2(c)Explain a Babcock and Wilcox boiler with a neat diagram.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

The traditional Babcock and Wilcox boiler is an externally fired, horizontal water-tube boiler.

Construction

It has an upper steam-and-water drum, inclined water tubes connected to headers, a furnace below/around the tube bank, baffles to guide hot gases and a flue path. Water and the steam-water mixture are inside the tubes; combustion gases flow outside them.

Working

  1. Fuel burns in the furnace and produces hot gases.
  2. Baffles direct the gases across the inclined tube bank, transferring heat through the tube walls to the water.
  3. The heated water/steam mixture rises through the circulation circuit; denser water returns along the designed path, supporting natural circulation in the conventional arrangement.
  4. Steam separates in the drum and is taken from the steam outlet. Remaining water circulates back through the tubes.

Compared with a basic fire-tube layout, a water-tube design can suit higher duties when properly engineered. The diagram is schematic, not a service drawing.

Q2(a)Write down three functions of a boiler.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  1. Generate steam: transfer heat from fuel or another heat source to feedwater and convert it into steam.
  2. Deliver a useful steam supply: provide steam at the required pressure, temperature, rate and quality for the connected process or machine.
  3. Contain and manage the process safely: provide a designed water/steam space, circulation and steam separation, with suitable controls, indication and protective devices to maintain permitted conditions.

Boiler construction, operation and protection must follow the certified design and authorised procedures.

Q2(b)Describe Charles's law.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Charles's law states that for a fixed mass of ideal gas at constant absolute pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature:

V ∝ T;   V/T = constant;   V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂.

Temperatures in the ratio must be in kelvin: T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15. For example, if absolute temperature increases by a factor of 1.5 at constant pressure, volume increases by the same factor. The law assumes the same gas mass/composition and an ideal-gas approximation.

Q2(c)Explain a Cochran boiler with a neat diagram.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

The traditional Cochran boiler is a vertical, multi-tubular, internally fired fire-tube boiler.

Construction

It consists of a vertical shell with an upper steam space, a furnace and grate in the lower part, a combustion chamber, fire tubes, a smoke box and a flue/chimney path. Water surrounds the furnace and the fire-tube heating surfaces.

Working

  1. Fuel burns on the grate; the hot products pass through the furnace and combustion chamber.
  2. The gases flow through the fire tubes, transferring heat through the metal to water surrounding the tubes.
  3. The cooled gases leave through the smoke box and flue. Heated water circulates; steam rises, separates from water and collects in the upper steam space.
  4. Steam is supplied through the designed outlet. Mountings provide indication, control and pressure protection.

It is compact in the traditional teaching comparison. Actual operating limits and configuration are set by the boiler's certified design.

Main question set — answer (a), (b) and (c)
Q3(a)Write the classification of pumps.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

The main pump classes are:

  • Positive-displacement pumps: move a trapped volume of fluid. They include reciprocating pumps (piston, plunger or diaphragm) and rotary pumps (gear, screw or vane).
  • Rotodynamic pumps: a rotating impeller continuously adds energy to the fluid. They include centrifugal/radial-flow, axial-flow and mixed-flow pumps.

Other descriptors include single-acting/double-acting, single-/multi-stage, and by application or liquid. Classification depends on the basis stated.

Q3(b)Explain, with a neat sketch, the working of a single-acting reciprocating pump.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A single-acting reciprocating pump has a piston in a cylinder, a crank-and-connecting-rod drive, a suction pipe with a non-return valve and a delivery pipe with a non-return valve. Liquid is pumped on only one side of the piston.

  1. Suction stroke: the piston moves away from the cylinder head; cylinder volume increases and pressure falls below suction pressure. The suction valve opens and liquid enters; the delivery valve is closed.
  2. Delivery stroke: the piston moves toward the head; volume decreases and pressure rises. The suction valve closes and the delivery valve opens, forcing liquid into the delivery pipe.

One suction and one delivery event occur per piston cycle, so flow is pulsating.

Q3(c)With a neat sketch, explain the working of a four-stroke petrol engine.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A conventional four-stroke petrol engine is a spark-ignition (SI) internal-combustion engine. The four strokes take two crankshaft revolutions and give one principal power stroke per cylinder.

  1. Suction / intake (TDC to BDC): the inlet valve opens; the descending piston draws in a fresh charge. In the conventional port-injection example this is an air-fuel mixture; some modern designs admit air and inject fuel separately.
  2. Compression (BDC to TDC): both valves are nominally closed; the rising piston compresses the charge. Near the end of compression, the spark plug ignites it.
  3. Power / expansion (TDC to BDC): hot expanding gases push the piston down, turning the crankshaft through the connecting rod. Both valves are nominally closed during the main expansion period.
  4. Exhaust (BDC to TDC): the exhaust valve opens and the rising piston pushes burnt gases out.

The flywheel helps carry the mechanism through the non-power strokes. A basic sketch omits real valve-timing advance, delay and overlap.

Q3(a)Define a pump and write its applications.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

A pump is a machine that transfers mechanical energy to a liquid to move it and/or raise its pressure or head. It converts input mechanical energy into hydraulic energy; flow through the system and its resistance determine the operating pressure.

Applications include domestic and municipal water supply, irrigation, boiler feed, drainage/dewatering, oil transfer, cooling-water circulation and process-fluid transfer. The pump must be selected for the liquid, flow, head and service conditions.

Q3(b)Explain, with a neat sketch, the working of a double-acting reciprocating pump.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A double-acting reciprocating pump has a piston dividing a cylinder into two working chambers. Each side has its own suction check valve connected to the suction supply and delivery check valve connected to a common delivery line.

  1. As the piston moves one way, the chamber whose volume increases draws liquid through its suction valve; at the same time the opposite chamber's volume decreases and it delivers liquid through its delivery valve.
  2. When the piston reverses, the valve roles reverse: the first side delivers while the second side draws in liquid.

Thus both piston strokes produce delivery, giving more continuous discharge than a single-acting pump (though flow still pulsates). Valves open according to pressure difference and prevent reverse flow.

Q3(c)With a neat sketch, explain the working of a four-stroke diesel engine.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A conventional diesel is a compression-ignition (CI) engine. The cycle uses four piston strokes and two crankshaft revolutions per cycle.

  1. Intake (TDC to BDC): the inlet valve opens and the descending piston draws in air only.
  2. Compression (BDC to TDC): valves are nominally closed. The piston compresses the air to a high pressure and temperature. Fuel is injected near the end of compression and self-ignites in the hot air.
  3. Power / expansion (TDC to BDC): combustion products expand and push the piston down, turning the crankshaft through the connecting rod.
  4. Exhaust (BDC to TDC): the exhaust valve opens and the rising piston expels burnt gases; the cycle then repeats.

Unlike a conventional petrol SI engine, a diesel does not use a spark plug to ignite the charge. Actual valve timing is more complex than the simplified sketch.

Main question set — answer (a), (b) and (c)
Q4(a)Write the modes of mechanical power transmission.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Common mechanical modes are:

  • Direct contact: gears and friction wheels transmit motion between contacting members; a coupling directly joins aligned shaft ends.
  • Flexible-element drive: belts or ropes over pulleys transmit power between separated shafts.
  • Positive flexible drive: a chain engaging sprockets transmits motion without ordinary friction-belt slip.

Each mode can transmit power while changing speed, torque or direction as designed. Losses occur, so a transmission does not create power.

Q4(b)Write the types of belt drive and explain a compound belt drive.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Belt types: flat, V, circular/rope and toothed (synchronous) belts. Common arrangements include open, crossed, quarter-turn, fast-and-loose, stepped/cone-pulley and compound drives.

Compound belt drive: a driver pulley D₁ drives an intermediate pulley D₂ through the first belt. A second pulley D₃ is fixed on the same intermediate shaft as D₂ and drives the final pulley D₄ through the second belt. The intermediate shaft therefore carries two pulleys and the two speed changes multiply. For an ideal no-slip arrangement with parallel shafts:

Nout/Nin = (D₁/D₂)(D₃/D₄).

Use effective/pitch diameters as appropriate. Real belt slip and losses make the actual ratio differ.

Q4(c)Describe, with a neat sketch, the construction and working of a single-stage reciprocating air compressor.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A single-stage reciprocating compressor raises air from inlet to delivery pressure in one compression stage.

Construction

It consists of a cylinder and head, piston and rings, piston rod, connecting rod and crankshaft, suction and delivery valves, a drive and suitable lubrication/cooling. A practical system may have an inlet filter, receiver, pressure gauge, control/unloader and certified relief protection.

Working

  1. Suction: piston moves to increase cylinder volume. Residual clearance air first re-expands; once cylinder pressure is below inlet pressure, the suction valve opens and air enters.
  2. Compression: the return stroke reduces volume and compresses trapped air; the suction valve closes.
  3. Delivery: when cylinder pressure becomes greater than delivery pressure, the delivery valve opens and compressed air flows to the receiver. Some clearance air remains at the end of travel.

Compression normally raises air temperature. A receiver is a separate pressure vessel; never exceed its rating or defeat its relief device.

Q4(a)List any three applications of mechanical power transmission.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • An electric motor drives a pump, fan or compressor through a belt, coupling or gearbox.
  • An engine transmits power through a gearbox and final drive to vehicle wheels.

Q4(b)Write the types of gear drive and explain a helical gear.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Types of gear drives: spur, helical, bevel, worm-and-wheel and rack-and-pinion. Spur and helical gears commonly serve parallel shafts; bevel gears commonly serve intersecting shafts; worm drives commonly serve non-parallel, non-intersecting shafts; a rack-and-pinion converts rotary to linear motion.

Helical gear: its teeth are cut at a helix angle to the shaft axis. Contact begins progressively along the tooth, so engagement is smoother and quieter than a comparable spur gear. More than one tooth pair may share load. The angled teeth create axial thrust, so shafts, bearings and housing must be designed to carry it. A usual parallel-shaft pair uses opposite-hand helices.

Q4(c)Describe, with the help of a neat sketch, the working principle of a block brake.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A block brake uses a friction block (shoe) pressed against the rim of a rotating drum or wheel.

  1. A lever or other designed actuator applies a force to the block.
  2. The block presses against the rotating surface with a normal force. Friction acts tangentially opposite the surface motion, producing a resisting torque.
  3. The torque reduces speed or stops the rotating member; the wheel's kinetic energy is mainly converted into heat in the lining and drum.
  4. When the actuating force is released, the block is withdrawn (often by a return spring) and free rotation can resume.

For a simple tangential-force model, braking torque is approximately Tb = Ffrictionr, where r is the effective drum radius. Actual force, direction effects, lining and heat rating require the brake design.

Q5(a)Write the types of material-handling equipment.3 marks⌄

  • Conveying: belt, roller, screw and bucket conveyors for movement along a route.

Q5(b)Explain a split air conditioner with a neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A typical split air conditioner has two main units connected by refrigerant lines and electrical/control links:

  • Indoor unit: evaporator coil and blower circulate room air over the cold coil. The refrigerant absorbs heat and evaporates; moisture may condense and is drained through the designed condensate arrangement.
  • Outdoor unit: compressor raises refrigerant pressure; condenser coil and fan reject heat to outdoor air and condense the refrigerant.
  • Expansion device: reduces refrigerant pressure before it returns to the indoor evaporator. Its exact location depends on the system design.

The refrigerant loop transports heat from indoors to outdoors. Installation and refrigerant work require qualified personnel and manufacturer-approved methods.

Q5(c)With a neat sketch, explain the flange coupling.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A flange coupling is a rigid coupling used to join two suitably aligned shafts and transmit torque.

Construction

A hub is mounted on each shaft and secured by a key (or a design-specific attachment). Each hub carries a flange. The two flange faces are brought together and bolted; bolts may be fitted or designed to transfer torque by the specified joint arrangement. A protective rim/cover may be provided.

Working

Torque passes from the driving shaft through its key and hub into the flange connection, across the bolted joint and through the second hub/key to the driven shaft. It provides a positive, rigid connection; it is not intended to accommodate arbitrary angular or parallel misalignment. Accurate alignment and a fitted guard are important.

Q5(b)Explain the vapour-compression refrigeration (VCR) system.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

The VCR system circulates refrigerant through four components:

  1. Evaporator: low-pressure refrigerant absorbs heat from the cooled space and vaporizes.
  2. Compressor: supplies work and raises the vapour to a high pressure and temperature.
  3. Condenser: rejects heat to ambient air/water and condenses the refrigerant to high-pressure liquid.
  4. Expansion device: throttles the liquid to evaporator pressure; the resulting cold liquid-vapour mixture returns to the evaporator.

Heat removed from the cooled space plus compressor work is rejected at the condenser. The cycle repeats continuously.

Q5(c)Draw and explain a centrifugal clutch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A centrifugal clutch is an automatically engaging friction clutch, often used in small-engine drives.

Construction

A driving hub on the engine shaft carries pivoted or guided shoes/weights with friction linings. Return springs pull the shoes inward. The shoes rotate inside a drum connected to the driven shaft.

Working

  1. At low speed, spring force holds the shoes clear of the drum, so little torque is transmitted.
  2. As speed rises, the outward centrifugal effect on the shoes (proportional to mass, radius and angular-speed squared in the simple model) increases.
  3. When the shoes contact the drum, friction transmits torque to the driven member; greater contact force allows the clutch to carry the designed load.
  4. As speed falls, the springs retract the shoes and the clutch disengages.

Engagement speed, torque and heat capacity depend on the design. It is not an overload safety device or a substitute for a brake.

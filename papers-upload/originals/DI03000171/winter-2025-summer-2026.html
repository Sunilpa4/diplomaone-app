Q1(a)Define : Compressibility, Capillarity and Density3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Compressibility, β: fractional decrease of volume per unit pressure rise: β = −(1/V)(dV/dp) = 1/K, where K is bulk modulus. SI unit: Pa⁻¹.
  • Capillarity: rise or depression of a liquid in a narrow tube caused by surface tension together with liquid–solid adhesion and liquid cohesion. For a clean circular tube, h = 4σ cosθ/(ρgd).
  • Density, ρ: mass per unit volume, ρ = m/V; SI unit kg/m³.
Q1(b)(i) Enlist assumptions of Bernoulli’s equation. (ii) Write the need of priming in centrifugal pump.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

(i) Bernoulli assumptions:

  • Flow is steady and the fluid is incompressible.
  • Viscous losses are negligible (ideal/inviscid flow).
  • The equation is applied along a streamline; it applies across streamlines if the flow is irrotational.
  • No pump or turbine shaft work, heat transfer, or other energy addition/removal occurs between the sections.
  • Velocity is represented by its section mean and kinetic-energy correction is taken as 1.
p/(ρg) + V²/(2g) + z = constant

(ii) Priming: before an ordinary centrifugal pump starts, fill its casing and suction line with liquid and remove trapped air. Air has very low density, so an air-filled impeller cannot establish the pressure difference needed to lift liquid into the suction pipe; priming allows the pump to develop suction and deliver flow. Flooded-suction or purpose-designed self-priming systems are exceptions.

Q1(c)State Pascal’s law of pressure and prove it mathematically.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Statement: a pressure increment applied to a confined fluid at rest is transmitted undiminished throughout the fluid and to the containing walls. Hydrostatic pressure still varies with elevation; Pascal’s law concerns the transmitted increment.

Mathematical proof at a point (pressure isotropy): consider an infinitesimal triangular fluid wedge in static equilibrium. Let the sloping face have area A and make angle θ with the horizontal. Its projected vertical and horizontal face areas are A cosθ and A sinθ. Let the corresponding pressures be pz, px and pn. In the limiting small wedge its weight is negligible compared with pressure forces because weight scales with volume, while pressure force scales with area.

Horizontal equilibrium: px(A sinθ) = pnA sinθ ⇒ px = pn
Vertical equilibrium: pz(A cosθ) = pnA cosθ ⇒ pz = pn

Thus pressure at a point is the same in every direction. In a static fluid, p₂ − p₁ = ρg(z₁ − z₂). Adding an imposed pressure increment Δp to one point adds that same increment at every other point, so Δp₂ = Δp₁. For two equal-height hydraulic pistons, F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂; force multiplication does not multiply energy.

Q2(b)With neat sketch explain working of a Venturimeter.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A Venturimeter measures discharge in a full pipe using the pressure fall produced by a smooth contraction.

  1. Construction: a converging inlet leads from pipe area A₁ to a short throat of area A₂; a gradual diffuser returns toward pipe size. Pressure taps at inlet and throat connect to a differential manometer or transducer.
  2. At steady flow, continuity gives A₁V₁ = A₂V₂ for an incompressible liquid, so velocity increases in the throat.
  3. Bernoulli’s equation then predicts lower static pressure at the throat; the pressure difference is read and converted to flowing-fluid head.
  4. Use the calibrated discharge coefficient Cd to account for real losses:
Q = CdA₂√[2gh/(1 − (A₂/A₁)²)]

It is accurate with comparatively low permanent head loss, but larger and more costly than an orifice meter.

Q2(c)The difference of heads between two ends of a pipe of 250 m length and 30 cm diameter is 1.5 m. Neglecting minor losses and taking Darcy’s co-efficient as 0.01, Calculate discharge in lit/sec through pipe.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Take the printed Darcy coefficient as the Darcy–Weisbach friction factor fD, as defined in the supplied course notes, and the full head difference as friction head hf.

hf = fD(L/D)(V²/2g)
V = √[2ghfD/(fDL)]
= √[2(9.81)(1.5)(0.30)/(0.01×250)] = 1.8793 m/s
A = πD²/4 = π(0.30)²/4 = 0.070686 m²
Q = AV = 0.070686(1.8793) = 0.13284 m³/s
= 132.84 L/s

Minor losses are excluded as instructed. If a different text uses its given coefficient as the Fanning factor fF, use 4fF in the Darcy equation; the convention must not be mixed.

Assumption / convention: Friction-factor convention: 0.01 is interpreted as Darcy fD, consistent with the course notes.

Q2(b)Explain Orifice meter with neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

An Orifice meter is a compact differential-pressure flow meter consisting of a thin, sharp-edged plate with a known opening fitted across a pipe.

  1. Upstream and downstream pressure taps connect to a manometer or pressure transducer.
  2. As liquid contracts through the opening and vena contracta, its velocity rises and static pressure falls.
  3. The measured pressure/head difference is related to discharge by continuity and Bernoulli’s equation.
  4. Apply the calibrated discharge coefficient to include contraction and losses; the downstream pressure partly recovers but permanent loss is appreciable.
Q = CdAo√[2gh/(1 − (Ao/A1)²)]

It is inexpensive and easy to replace, but is more sensitive to edge condition and generally loses more head than a Venturimeter.

Q3(a)Define: Stream line, Path line and Stream tube3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Streamline: at a specified instant, a curve everywhere tangent to the local velocity vector. No fluid crosses a streamline at that instant.
  • Pathline: the actual trajectory followed by one identified fluid particle over time.
  • Stream tube: a tube-like control surface bounded by streamlines; its sides are impermeable to instantaneous cross-flow. In steady flow, streamline and pathline coincide.
Q3(b)Describe Reynold’s experiment with neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Apparatus: a constant-level water tank, smooth transparent test pipe of known diameter, dye reservoir with fine injector, downstream flow-control valve, and a flow-measuring tank or meter.

  1. Establish a gentle, steady flow through the transparent tube.
  2. Introduce a thin dye filament at the pipe inlet without disturbing the water.
  3. At low velocity the filament remains nearly straight and distinct: flow is laminar.
  4. Increase flow gradually: the filament begins to oscillate and then mixes across the pipe, indicating transition and turbulent flow.
  5. Measure discharge Q; calculate mean V = Q/A and Reynolds number Re = ρVD/μ = VD/ν. For a circular pipe, Re below about 2300 is commonly laminar, about 2300–4000 transitional, and above about 4000 commonly turbulent; disturbances affect the boundaries.
Q3(c)Derive equation of force, work done and efficiency due to impact of jet on series of moving flat plate.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Let a water jet of area a and absolute velocity V strike a continuous series of flat plates moving in the jet direction at speed u (0 ≤ u < V). Assume the series intercepts the complete jet, the fluid leaves the plate with axial absolute velocity u, and losses outside the plate interaction are neglected.

  1. Because successive plates catch the whole jet, mass flow through the series is ṁ = ρaV.
  2. Inlet and outlet axial velocities are V and u. By momentum change, force on the plates is:
F = ṁ(V−u) = ρaV(V−u)
  1. Work rate (power) delivered to the moving plates is force times plate speed:
P = Fu = ρaV(V−u)u
Work in time t: W = Pt
  1. Jet kinetic-energy rate at the nozzle is Pjet = ½ρaV³. Therefore the efficiency based on the whole jet is:
η = P/Pjet = 2u(V−u)/V²

Let x = u/V. Then η = 2x(1−x); dη/dx = 2−4x = 0 gives x = ½. Thus optimum plate speed u = V/2 and maximum efficiency ηmax = 0.50 = 50%. A series plate differs from one isolated plate because it captures all of the jet mass flow.

Q3(a)Define: Co-efficient of contraction, Co-efficient of discharge, Co-efficient of velocity3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Coefficient of contraction, Cc: area of the jet at vena contracta divided by orifice area: Cc = Ac/Ao.
  • Coefficient of velocity, Cv: actual jet velocity at vena contracta divided by ideal Torricelli velocity: Cv = Vactual/√(2gH).
  • Coefficient of discharge, Cd: actual discharge divided by ideal discharge based on orifice area: Cd = Qactual/[Ao√(2gH)].

For a sharp-edged orifice with the same head reference, Cd = CcCv.

Q3(b)Write short note on “Water hammer”.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Water hammer is a transient pressure wave produced when flow velocity in a liquid-filled pipe changes rapidly. Rapid valve closure/opening, pump trip or check-valve slam can produce high or low pressure surges.

  • It results from liquid compressibility and elastic expansion of the pipe; wave speed c depends on both.
  • For a rapid velocity change, the ideal Joukowsky relation is Δp = ρcΔV; an abrupt arrest can therefore create a large surge.
  • Effects include noise, vibration, pipe/support loads, leakage, rupture or column separation followed by impact.
  • Control by suitable valve-closing time, surge tank or air vessel, pressure-relief/surge devices, proper check valves and rated pipe supports. Design must consider the full transient, not just steady head.
Q3(c)Derive equation of force, work done and efficiency due to impact of jet on a flat vertical moving plate.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Let a jet of area a and speed V strike a single flat plate moving away in the jet direction at speed u, with 0 ≤ u < V. Assume the jet is completely intercepted across the plate, the water leaves with axial absolute velocity u, and density is ρ.

  1. The jet meets the moving plate at relative speed V−u, so the intercepted mass rate is ṁ = ρa(V−u).
  2. The axial velocity change of this intercepted water is V−u. Thus:
F = ṁ(V−u) = ρa(V−u)²
  1. Work rate delivered to the plate is:
P = Fu = ρa(V−u)²u
Work in time t: W = Pt
  1. Using the full nozzle jet power Pjet = ½ρaV³, the conventional overall jet efficiency is:
ηjet = P/Pjet = 2u(V−u)²/V³

With x = u/V, η = 2x(1−x)²; dη/dx = 2(1−x)(1−3x), so maximum overall jet efficiency occurs at u = V/3 and equals 8/27 = 29.63%. If efficiency is instead defined only on the kinetic-energy rate of the mass already intercepted, ηintercepted = 2u(V−u)/V², whose maximum is 50% at u = V/2. State the chosen energy basis.

Q4(a)Differentiate: Steady flow and Unsteady flow3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Steady flowUnsteady flow
At a fixed point, fluid properties (such as velocity, pressure and density) do not change with time: ∂()/∂t = 0.At a fixed point, one or more fluid properties change with time: ∂()/∂t ≠ 0.
Mass flow at a section remains constant with time, though it can vary from section to section in a spatially varying field.Mass stored in a control volume can change with time; instantaneous readings vary.
Example: established flow in a pipe supplied at constant conditions.Example: draining tank, pump start-up or a water-hammer transient.

Steady is a time classification; it does not necessarily mean uniform in space.

Q4(b)A jet of water, 3 cm diameter, strikes a flat plate in normal direction with a velocity of 35 m/s. Find the force exerted by jet if (i) the plate is stationary (ii) the plate moves with velocity 15 m/s in the direction of jet.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Take ρ = 1000 kg/m³, jet area a = πd²/4 = π(0.03)²/4 = 0.00070686 m². The jet is turned sideways at a flat plate.

(i) Stationary plate: F = ρaV²
= 1000(0.00070686)(35²) = 865.9 N
(ii) Moving plate: relative speed V−u = 35−15 = 20 m/s; intercepted ṁ = ρa(V−u).
F = ρa(V−u)² = 1000(0.00070686)(20²) = 282.7 N

For the moving plate, only the relative stream rate reaches this single plate; do not use the full nozzle mass rate.

Q4(c)Explain construction and working of Centrifugal pump with neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Construction: an impeller with curved vanes is mounted on a rotating shaft inside a volute or diffuser casing. The impeller eye connects to the suction pipe; the delivery opening connects to the delivery pipe. A foot valve/strainer may be fitted at the suction inlet to retain liquid and exclude debris. Bearings, seals, base and prime mover support the unit.

Working:

  1. Prime the casing and suction pipe where the installation is not flooded-suction/self-priming.
  2. Start the impeller under the approved procedure; liquid is thrown radially outward by the rotating vanes.
  3. Pressure near the eye falls, so atmospheric/reservoir pressure drives more liquid through the suction pipe into the impeller.
  4. Liquid gains velocity and pressure while moving through the impeller; the casing collects the flow and converts part of velocity head to pressure head.
  5. Pressurised liquid leaves through the delivery pipe. The process continues while speed, suction conditions and delivery system permit.

The impeller supplies energy to the fluid. Avoid running dry; cavitation, air leakage and inadequate net positive suction head reduce performance and can damage the pump.

Q4(c)Explain construction and working of Pelton wheel turbine with neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A Pelton wheel is an impulse turbine suited to high head and comparatively low discharge. Its runner operates in a ventilated casing at approximately atmospheric pressure.

Construction: penstock and nozzle; spear/needle or deflector to regulate jet; runner disc on shaft; double-cup buckets with a central splitter around the rim; bearings, casing and free-discharge tailrace.

Working:

  1. Net head is converted to a high-speed jet by the nozzle.
  2. The jet strikes each bucket tangentially at its splitter and divides into two streams.
  3. Curved bucket surfaces turn the relative flow nearly backward, reducing its tangential momentum.
  4. The change in water momentum exerts tangential force and torque on the runner.
  5. The runner shaft transmits mechanical power to the generator; spent water leaves freely through the casing.
  6. A spear/deflector controls flow and load response; suitable governing limits water-hammer risk in the penstock.

There is no draft tube on a Pelton turbine. Bucket outlet angle, speed ratio, head and losses determine actual efficiency.

Q5(a)State the purpose of air vessel in reciprocating pump.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

An air vessel is a pressure-rated chamber near the pump cylinder (on the suction and/or delivery line) with a compressible air cushion above the liquid. It:

  • stores liquid when instantaneous piston delivery exceeds the line-average flow and returns it when delivery falls;
  • smooths pulsating discharge and makes pipeline velocity more uniform;
  • reduces acceleration head and friction loss in the long suction/delivery pipes, and can reduce required work and separation risk.

It buffers flow; it does not create energy or make operation beyond the pump’s rated conditions safe.

Q5(b)Draw a line diagram of a hydro-electric power plant and label its main parts.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A typical layout, from upstream to downstream, is:

  1. Reservoir and dam: store water and create gross head.
  2. Trash rack and intake gate: screen debris and control entry.
  3. Headrace/forebay: convey and regulate water before the pressure conduit.
  4. Surge tank: moderates pressure transients where provided.
  5. Penstock: carries pressurised water to the turbine.
  6. Powerhouse: contains turbine, shaft, generator and controls.
  7. Draft tube (reaction turbine) and tailrace: recover part of outlet velocity head and return water downstream.

The generator output is connected to a transformer and electrical grid; label these if included in the line diagram.

Q5(a)Explain terms: (i) Positive displacement pump (ii) Rotodynamic pump3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Positive-displacement pump: traps a finite liquid volume and forces it from inlet to outlet each cycle or revolution. Examples: reciprocating piston/plunger and rotary gear pumps. It is suited to controlled flow/high pressure; relief protection is important because a blocked discharge can create excessive pressure.
  • Rotodynamic pump: a rotating impeller continuously transfers energy to flowing liquid, raising velocity and pressure. Examples: centrifugal, mixed-flow and axial-flow pumps. Discharge depends on the pump/system operating point; priming or flooded suction may be required.
Q5(b)Write short note on draft tube.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A draft tube is a sealed, gradually expanding diffuser between the runner outlet of a reaction turbine and the tailrace.

  • Its increasing area reduces mean outlet velocity and converts part of the velocity head into pressure head.
  • It permits the runner to be set above tailwater while maintaining a continuous water column and recovering some outlet energy.
  • Types include straight conical, simple elbow, elbow with varying cross-section and bell-mouthed/hydra-cone forms.
  • It must be designed to avoid excessive separation, loss, air entry and cavitation; recovery is less than ideal because losses remain.

Pelton is an impulse turbine discharging to atmosphere and does not use a draft tube.

Q1(a)Explain surface tension with neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Surface tension σ is the tangential tensile force per unit length acting at a liquid interface (equivalently, interfacial energy per unit area for a simple interface). It arises from molecular cohesion and tends to reduce free-surface area, which is why a small liquid drop tends toward a spherical shape.

σ = F/L; SI unit = N/m

It is an interfacial force, not a pressure throughout the liquid. For an ideal spherical liquid drop with one interface, excess pressure is 4σ/d; a soap bubble has two interfaces and 8σ/d.

Q1(b)Explain impact of jet on moving curved vane when jet striking at the center.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Let a jet of area a and velocity V strike the centre of a single curved vane moving in the jet direction at speed u. Assume the vane divides the stream symmetrically into two equal outlets; the transverse momentum components cancel. Let relative outlet speed W₂ = kW₁, with W₁ = V−u, and let β be measured from the backward direction of the incident jet.

Absolute outlet x-component: Vw2 = u − k(V−u)cosβ
Mass rate intercepted by one moving vane: ṁ = ρa(V−u)
Force in the direction of vane motion:
F = ṁ(V−Vw2) = ρa(V−u)²(1+k cosβ)
Power delivered = Fu

For a frictionless vane k≈1; real friction reduces relative outlet speed. The central strike and symmetric split are why side forces cancel. For a continuous series catching the full jet, use ṁ = ρaV instead; with the same angle convention Fx = ρaV(V−u)(1+k cosβ), and P = Fxu. The formula above is for one isolated moving vane.

Q1(a)Explain surface tension with neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Surface tension σ is the tangential tensile force per unit length acting at a liquid interface (equivalently, interfacial energy per unit area for a simple interface). It arises from molecular cohesion and tends to reduce free-surface area, which is why a small liquid drop tends toward a spherical shape.

σ = F/L; SI unit = N/m

It is an interfacial force, not a pressure throughout the liquid. For an ideal spherical liquid drop with one interface, excess pressure is 4σ/d; a soap bubble has two interfaces and 8σ/d.

Q1(b)Explain impact of jet on moving curved vane when jet striking at the center.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Let a jet of area a and velocity V strike the centre of a single curved vane moving in the jet direction at speed u. Assume the vane divides the stream symmetrically into two equal outlets; the transverse momentum components cancel. Let relative outlet speed W₂ = kW₁, with W₁ = V−u, and let β be measured from the backward direction of the incident jet.

Absolute outlet x-component: Vw2 = u − k(V−u)cosβ
Mass rate intercepted by one moving vane: ṁ = ρa(V−u)
Force in the direction of vane motion:
F = ṁ(V−Vw2) = ρa(V−u)²(1+k cosβ)
Power delivered = Fu

For a frictionless vane k≈1; real friction reduces relative outlet speed. The central strike and symmetric split are why side forces cancel. For a continuous series catching the full jet, use ṁ = ρaV instead; with the same angle convention Fx = ρaV(V−u)(1+k cosβ), and P = Fxu. The formula above is for one isolated moving vane.

Q1(c)State and prove Pascal's law.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Law: a pressure increment imposed on a confined fluid at rest is transmitted equally throughout the fluid and to the containing walls. Total hydrostatic pressure may still differ with elevation.

Proof using a small fluid wedge: let the inclined face have area A and angle θ to horizontal; its projected horizontal and vertical areas are A sinθ and A cosθ. Let pn act on the inclined face and px, pz on the other two faces. Neglect wedge weight in the infinitesimal limit.

Horizontal force balance: pxA sinθ = pnA sinθ ⇒ px = pn
Vertical force balance: pzA cosθ = pnA cosθ ⇒ pz = pn

Thus pressure at a point is isotropic. Hydrostatic balance gives p₂−p₁ = ρg(z₁−z₂). Adding the same external increment to the confined liquid leaves this elevation relation unchanged, hence Δp₂ = Δp₁. In a hydraulic press at equal elevations, F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂.

Q2(b)Define: 1. Compressible flow, 2. unsteady flow4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

  1. Compressible flow: a flow in which density change is significant for the analysis, typically because pressure/temperature changes appreciably. Incompressible is an approximation of nearly constant density, not a different fluid.
  2. Unsteady flow: at a fixed location, one or more properties such as velocity, pressure or density vary with time; mathematically, a temporal derivative such as ∂V/∂t is non-zero. A draining tank and pump start-up are examples.

Compressible describes density behavior; unsteady describes time variation. They are independent classifications.

Q2(c)State and derive the continuity equation.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Statement: mass is conserved. For steady flow through a stream tube, mass flow entering equals mass flow leaving.

  1. Consider a short time interval Δt and one inlet section (1) and one outlet section (2).
  2. Mass entering = ρ₁A₁V₁Δt; mass leaving = ρ₂A₂V₂Δt, where V is the mean velocity normal to the section.
  3. For steady flow with no accumulation inside the control volume, equate these masses and divide by Δt:
ρ₁A₁V₁ = ρ₂A₂V₂ = constant mass-flow rate ṁ

For an incompressible fluid ρ₁ = ρ₂ = ρ:

A₁V₁ = A₂V₂ = Q

This assumes no side branches or leakage between sections; with multiple inlets/outlets, sum all mass flow rates algebraically.

Q2(a)State limitation of piezometer.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • It measures only positive gauge pressure of a liquid; it cannot measure gas pressure or vacuum/negative gauge pressure.
  • For high pressure the required liquid column would be impractically tall.
  • It is unsuitable where the flowing liquid is volatile, toxic, very viscous, or where capillary/meniscus and reading effects are significant.
  • It gives pressure at one tap, not a direct differential reading between two points.
Q2(b)Write Bernoulli's equation. Also write assumption, and limitations of Bernoulli's theorem.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

For ideal steady incompressible flow along a streamline:

p/(ρg) + V²/(2g) + z = constant

The terms are pressure head, velocity head and elevation head.

Assumptions: steady flow; constant density; negligible viscosity/energy loss; no pump/turbine work or heat addition between sections; applied along a streamline (everywhere only for irrotational flow); mean velocity and kinetic-energy factor α≈1.

Limitations: ideal Bernoulli alone is not valid across significant friction, turbulence-related losses, pumps/turbines, heat transfer, shocks, or materially compressible flow. For real systems use the extended energy equation with pump head, turbine head and head loss.

Q3(a)Explain following Hydraulic coefficient: (1) Co efficient of contraction, (2) Coefficient of velocity, (3) Coefficient of discharge.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Cc = Ac/Ao: vena-contracta jet area divided by orifice area.
  • Cv = Vactual/√(2gH): actual jet speed divided by the ideal speed from Torricelli’s theorem.
  • Cd = Qactual/[Ao√(2gH)]: actual discharge divided by ideal discharge.

For a sharp-edged orifice under the same head, Cd = CcCv; contraction and velocity loss both reduce actual flow.

Q3(b)What is priming? Explain different methods of priming.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Priming is filling the casing and suction pipe of an ordinary centrifugal pump with liquid and expelling air before start-up. An air-filled pump cannot create sufficient suction head because air is much less dense than the pumped liquid.

  • Manual filling and venting: pour liquid into the casing through a priming opening while allowing air to escape; a foot/check valve retains the column.
  • Vacuum priming: a vacuum pump or air ejector removes air so liquid rises from the source into the casing.
  • Flooded suction: locate the pump below the source liquid level so casing and suction remain full.
  • Self-priming design: a purpose-designed casing retains a liquid reserve and recirculates it to evacuate air. This is not a property of every centrifugal pump.

Prime and operate only according to the pump’s approved procedure; never run a conventional pump dry.

Q3(c)What is Reynold’s Number? Explain Reynold’s experiment with sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Reynolds number is a dimensionless ratio of inertial to viscous effects. For a circular pipe:

Re = ρVD/μ = VD/ν

Here V is mean pipe velocity, D is inside diameter, μ is dynamic viscosity and ν = μ/ρ is kinematic viscosity.

Experiment: feed water from a constant-level tank through a smooth transparent pipe; introduce a fine dye filament at the inlet and regulate discharge with a valve. At low flow the dye remains a straight filament (laminar). With increasing flow it wavers and then diffuses across the pipe (transition/turbulent). Measure Q and D, find V = Q/(πD²/4), and calculate Re. For ordinary circular-pipe flow, Re below about 2300 is commonly laminar; 2300–4000 transitional; above about 4000 commonly turbulent. Disturbances and entrance conditions affect transition.

Q3(a)Explain Horizontal Venturimeter with neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

A horizontal Venturimeter has a converging inlet, short throat and gradually expanding diffuser in a horizontal pipe. Incompressible continuity makes velocity rise from inlet to throat; Bernoulli then gives lower static pressure at the throat. A differential manometer connects the pressure taps. For known areas and Cd, discharge follows:

Q = CdA₂√[2gh/(1−(A₂/A₁)²)]

Because both sections are at the same elevation, the elevation-head terms cancel. The diffuser recovers part of the pressure head.

Q3(b)Write use of air-vessel in reciprocating pump with neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

An air vessel is a pressure chamber near the reciprocating-pump cylinder containing an air cushion over liquid.

  1. During a stroke interval when cylinder delivery exceeds line-average demand, surplus liquid enters the vessel and compresses the air.
  2. When instantaneous delivery falls, the compressed air expands and returns liquid to the pipe.
  3. The suction-side vessel similarly supplies part of the fluctuating cylinder demand.
  4. Result: pipeline flow becomes more uniform; acceleration head, friction loss, required work and separation risk in long pipes can be reduced.

It smooths pulsation rather than eliminating all flow variation. The vessel and gas charge must be rated and maintained for the operating pressure.

Q4(a)Describe working of Prandtl’s pitot tube with neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

A Prandtl (Pitot-static) tube measures local flow speed from the difference between stagnation pressure and static pressure.

  1. The forward-facing opening brings the fluid to rest at a stagnation point and senses total pressure.
  2. Small side holes, aligned with the flow, sense static pressure.
  3. A differential manometer/transducer measures Δp = p0 − p.
  4. For an incompressible liquid with negligible losses, Bernoulli gives V = √(2Δp/ρ). If a same-liquid manometer reads head h, V = √(2gh); apply the appropriate density correction for a different manometric fluid.

Align the probe with the flow and use its calibration; blockage, turbulence and misalignment affect readings.

Q5(a)Explain impact of jet on a moving flat plate held normal to the direction of jet.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Let a jet of area a, speed V and density ρ strike a single flat plate normally while it moves in the jet direction at speed u (u < V). Water leaves sideways, so its absolute axial velocity becomes u.

Intercepted mass rate ṁ = ρa(V−u)
Axial momentum change per second gives force:
F = ṁ(V−u) = ρa(V−u)²
Power delivered to the plate = Fu

Force acts in the direction of plate motion. For a stationary plate set u = 0 and recover F = ρaV². A continuous series of plates intercepts the full jet and uses ṁ = ρaV instead.

Take x along a jet of area a, speed V and mass rate ṁ = ρaV. A stationary curved vane struck at its centre divides the jet symmetrically. If the outlet relative speed is kV and β is measured from the reverse jet direction, each outlet has x-component −kV cosβ; transverse components cancel.

Q5(c)Discuss construction and working of pelton wheel turbine using neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A Pelton wheel is an impulse turbine for high head and comparatively low discharge.

Construction: a penstock supplies a nozzle fitted with a spear/needle; the jet is directed tangentially onto a runner with double-cup buckets and a central splitter. Runner and shaft are supported by bearings inside a ventilated splash casing; a free outlet returns water to the tailrace. A deflector/governor controls the jet/load response.

Working:

  1. The nozzle converts head into a high-velocity free jet.
  2. The jet strikes a bucket splitter and divides into two streams.
  3. Curved bucket faces turn the streams through a large angle, changing their tangential momentum.
  4. Momentum change creates force and torque on the runner.
  5. The runner drives the shaft/generator; water leaves at approximately atmospheric pressure and drains freely.
  6. The spear adjusts flow and the deflector can divert the jet during load changes; control transients to avoid penstock surge.

Pelton has no pressure-filled runner or draft tube; a reaction turbine such as Francis/Kaplan uses a draft tube.

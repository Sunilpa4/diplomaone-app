Q1(a)Differentiate between hot rolling and cold rolling process.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Q1(b)What is an electrode? Explain consumable and non-consumable electrodes.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

An electrode is the current-carrying member that establishes or sustains an arc (or resistance-welding contact) between the power source and work. In arc processes it may also supply filler metal.

  • Consumable electrode: melts and is transferred into the weld pool, supplying filler as well as carrying current. Examples: flux-coated SMAW rod and continuously fed MIG/MAG wire.
  • Non-consumable electrode: is not intended to melt as deposited filler; it carries current and creates the arc. Example: tungsten in TIG/GTAW. Separate filler rod may be added if the joint needs it. The electrode can still wear or require replacement.

Electrode selection must suit the base metal, process and approved welding procedure.

Q1(c)Mark the different zones on a sketch of a cupola furnace and explain each zone.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A cupola is a vertical, coke-fired shaft furnace commonly used for continuous melting of cast iron. Alternate metallic charge, coke and suitable flux descend as hot gases rise. Mark these approximate zones from the top downward:

  1. Stack / preheating zone: ascending hot gases heat the incoming charge before it reaches the melting region.
  2. Melting zone: the descending metallic charge softens and melts as it contacts the hot coke bed.
  3. Reducing zone: above the tuyeres, carbon monoxide-rich gases interact with the charge; boundaries vary with operating conditions.
  4. Combustion (oxidising) zone: at tuyere level, the air blast supports coke combustion (C + O₂ → CO₂), producing intense heat; reactions nearby also generate CO.
  5. Well / hearth: below the tuyeres, liquid iron collects and slag floats above it. The slag outlet is above the iron tap hole.

Zone limits are not sharp. The diagram also labels the charging door, stack, wind belt/tuyeres, outlets, refractory shell and bottom doors. No operating ratios or temperatures are supplied.

Q1(c)List the various types of rolling mills. Explain any one rolling mill with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Types: two-high (reversing or non-reversing), three-high, four-high, cluster/multi-high, tandem/continuous, universal and planetary mills.

Four-high mill: two small-diameter work rolls contact and reduce the strip. Two larger backup rolls support the work rolls against bending, allowing thinner strip and improved thickness control without making the work rolls excessively large. Bearings/chocks locate the rolls; a stand resists separating force, drives turn the rolls in opposite directions, and screw-down/gap controls set the reduction. The work passes through the roll bite; successive passes or stands reach the required thickness.

Mill choice depends on material, width, thickness, reduction, production rate and required flatness. The drawing compares common roll arrangements; the four-high arrangement is the right-hand group.

Q2(a)List the hand tools used in preparing a mould.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Riddle: screens sand and removes lumps.
  • Shovel: mixes/transfers moulding sand.
  • Rammer: compacts sand around the pattern.
  • Strike-off bar: levels excess sand at the flask face.
  • Vent wire: makes gas passages without damaging the cavity.
  • Trowel/slick and lifter: finish or repair surfaces and reach deeper mould areas.
  • Draw spike, swab and mallet: assist pattern withdrawal and protect/loosen the mould as appropriate.

Tools are selected and handled to avoid over-ramming, tearing the cavity or creating dust and lifting hazards.

Q2(b)List the various pattern allowances and explain them with a neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Pattern allowances:

  1. Shrinkage/contraction: usually positive; enlarges pattern dimensions to compensate solid-metal contraction on cooling.
  2. Machining/finishing: extra stock on surfaces that will be machined off.
  3. Draft: taper on withdrawal faces to release the pattern without tearing sand.
  4. Distortion/camber: intentional counter-shape to offset predictable warping of an asymmetric casting.
  5. Rapping/shake: commonly negative; pattern loosening during withdrawal enlarges the mould cavity.
  6. Mould-wall movement: process-dependent correction for mould enlargement under heat/metal pressure.

Allowances are applied by surface and direction; they are not all positive additions. Core prints locate/support a core but are not a seventh dimensional allowance.

Q2(c)Select the appropriate casting method to produce a hollow pipe and explain it.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Selected method: true centrifugal casting. It is suited to hollow cylindrical products such as pipes, sleeves and liners because rotation forms the bore without a sand core.

  1. A strong, balanced horizontal cylindrical mould is lined/coated and mounted on supports/drive rollers inside a guarded machine.
  2. The mould rotates at its specified controlled speed; qualified personnel introduce measured molten metal through the designed pouring arrangement.
  3. Centrifugal action holds the liquid against the mould wall. The mould defines the outside diameter while the free inner surface forms the hollow bore.
  4. Heat is extracted and metal solidifies from the outer wall inward; rotation is maintained for the required solidification stage.
  5. After controlled cooling and stopping, remove the casting safely, trim/machine ends and inspect bore, wall thickness and soundness.

Advantages include no core for the bore and good cylindrical integrity. The method needs specialised balanced equipment, is mainly for rotational shapes, and lighter inclusions may concentrate at the inner surface. Semi-centrifugal casting makes solid rotational parts; centrifuging fills off-axis cavities, so neither is the same pipe method.

Q2(a)Explain a gating system with a neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

A gating system is the controlled passage that carries molten metal from the pouring basin into the mould cavity. The basin receives the stream; a tapered sprue conducts it downward; a sprue well reduces impact; the runner distributes flow; ingates connect the runner to the cavity. Runner extensions/filters may trap inclusions. Vents release mould gas. A riser is a feeder for solidification shrinkage, not a gate.

Design balances filling time, erosion, aspiration, turbulence and premature freezing; simply enlarging every passage is not a sound design rule.

Q2(b)List the various types of pattern. Explain any one of them.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Types: single-piece, split/two-piece, multi-piece, loose-piece, match-plate, cope-and-drag, gated, sweep, skeleton, segmental and follow-board patterns.

Split pattern: the pattern is divided at a suitable parting plane into cope and drag halves. Locating pins/dowels align the halves during moulding. After each half has formed its portion of the cavity, the pattern is withdrawn without requiring a one-direction pull through the entire shape. It suits components whose geometry would make a single-piece pattern difficult to withdraw.

Pattern type is selected for shape, undercuts, parting line, withdrawal direction and production quantity; extra pieces improve access but add alignment/handling demands.

Q3(b)Discuss the importance of safety and various safety equipment in metal-joining processes.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Joining can expose workers to arc UV/IR, hot metal, spatter, fumes, electric shock, fire, compressed gas and pinch/crush hazards. Controls prevent burns, eye injury, respiratory exposure and serious fires.

  • Welding helmet with the correct filter, safety glasses and screens protect eyes/nearby workers from arc radiation and spatter.
  • Flame-resistant clothing, welding gloves and safety footwear protect skin from heat and sparks.
  • Local fume extraction/ventilation and a suitable respirator where the risk assessment requires it control fumes.
  • Insulated, rated leads/holders, sound connections, safe isolation and a correctly connected work return reduce shock risk; the return is not protective earthing.
  • For gas work: secure cylinders upright, use gas-specific regulators/hoses and specified non-return/flashback protection; keep oxygen equipment oil-free.
  • Remove combustibles, use the hot-work permit/fire watch and suitable fire equipment; never weld sealed/unknown containers.

Only trained personnel should set or operate equipment; follow the site procedure, extraction and guarding requirements.

Q3(c)Select the appropriate welding process to join a rail track and explain it.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Selected process: thermit (aluminothermic) rail welding. It is suitable for field rail joints where a moulded metal bridge is required and a conventional arc source is not the chosen route.

  1. Qualified personnel align the rail ends to the approved gap/profile and secure them with suitable fixtures.
  2. A dry refractory mould is fitted around the joint and sealed/checked; the qualified process may require controlled preheating.
  3. An approved proprietary thermit system generates superheated molten steel through an exothermic reduction reaction (commonly represented by Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat). No charge ratio or ignition procedure is assumed here.
  4. The molten metal flows into the moulded rail gap while slag is separated by the designed delivery arrangement.
  5. After the specified solidification/cooling stage, trained staff remove the mould, trim/grind the excess and finish the rail profile.
  6. Inspect alignment, surface profile and specified internal/acceptance criteria before the track is returned to service.

It produces a continuous field joint without a separate external arc source, but is a one-shot, extremely high-temperature specialist operation requiring a qualified procedure and exclusion controls.

Q3(b)Sketch the types of oxy-acetylene flames, showing clearly the different zones.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Neutral flame: approximately balanced oxygen/acetylene; it has a distinct inner cone and surrounding outer envelope, with no acetylene feather. It is used for many general welding applications.

Carburising (reducing) flame: excess acetylene; it shows an inner cone, a visible acetylene feather, and the outer envelope. Use only when compatible with the alloy/procedure because carbon pickup can change metal chemistry.

Oxidising flame: excess oxygen; it has a shorter, sharper inner cone and outer envelope. It can oxidise many metals and is selected only for suitable materials/processes.

The relative zone lengths in the sketch are schematic. Flame choice is by work material and qualified procedure, not by colour alone.

Q4(a)What is cold working process? State its advantages.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Cold working is plastic deformation carried out below the metal's recrystallisation range (often near ambient temperature). It changes shape without melting or chip removal and raises dislocation density through strain hardening.

  • Improved surface finish and closer dimensional accuracy because there is little high-temperature scale.
  • Increased strength and hardness from work hardening.
  • Good productivity and repeatability for suitable operations such as cold rolling, drawing and forming.
  • Material is not heated to forging/rolling temperature, though the forming force is higher and ductility can decrease.

Intermediate annealing may be needed after substantial reduction.

Q4(b)Explain the wire-drawing process with a neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Wire drawing reduces a rod/wire cross-section by pulling it in tension through a converging die; it is not extrusion, which pushes metal through a die.

  1. Clean and prepare drawable stock; lubricate it using the specified method.
  2. Point/thread the starting end through the die under safe setup conditions and grip it in a drawbench/capstan.
  3. Pull the stock through the die. The die land sets the smaller section; length increases as cross-sectional area decreases.
  4. Use further planned passes and intermediate annealing if required by material strain; inspect size, surface and straightness.

The die has an entry/bell, reduction zone, bearing and exit. Drawing gives good finish/tolerance but is limited by tensile force, ductility, die wear and cracking risk.

Q4(c)Select the appropriate forging method to manufacture a connecting rod and explain it.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Selected method: impression-die (closed-die) hot forging, usually in a press or drop-forging sequence. It is suited to repeated connecting-rod production because the die impressions form the web, big end and small end with controlled grain flow and good strength.

  1. Cut a billet of suitable alloy and calculate stock including specified scale/flash allowances.
  2. Heat/condition the billet to the approved forging range and place it in a blocker/preform impression.
  3. Apply controlled blows/press force to distribute metal toward the rod ends and web without folds or underfill.
  4. Transfer to the finishing impression; matched dies shape the rod outline and excess metal forms designed flash.
  5. Remove and trim flash using the planned tool; cool and heat-treat only as specified.
  6. Inspect fill, alignment, cracks and grain-flow-sensitive regions; machine the big- and small-end bores and other finished surfaces.

Repeatability and favourable worked structure are advantages; dies, trimming, high loads and stock-volume control add cost. Exact die stages depend on rod design and production route.

Q4(a)Classify the methods of metal-forging process.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Forging methods can be classified on more than one basis:

  • By die/tooling: open-die (smith), impression/closed-die, precision/flashless.
  • By loading/operation: hammer/drop forging, press forging, upset/heading forging and roll forging.
  • By temperature: hot, warm or cold forging, relative to material and process requirements.

These categories overlap: a closed-die forging can be made by hammer or press, and hot/cold describes temperature rather than die shape.

Q4(b)List the sequence of operations for manufacturing a hexagonal bolt by smith forging.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

  1. Cut round bar stock to the required blank length, including head allowance and finishing/thread allowance.
  2. Heat the head end uniformly to the approved forging condition; hold with suitable tongs.
  3. Upset the end to gather enough metal for the head; avoid folds and excessive scale.
  4. Use hammer blows and a suitable heading tool/swage block to form and size the hexagonal head, rotating the stock to form all flats.
  5. Draw/size the shank to the specified diameter and length; form the chamfer/point as required.
  6. Dress/trim, cool by the approved route, clean and inspect dimensions and cracks.
  7. Cut/roll the thread after forging as specified; threading is a later finishing operation, not a forged feature unless the drawing says otherwise.

Q5(a)List the properties of plastic materials.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Low density/light weight and easy mouldability into complex shapes.
  • Generally good corrosion and chemical resistance; properties vary by resin/grade.
  • Usually good electrical insulation and low thermal conductivity.
  • Many plastics have lower stiffness/strength and lower service temperature than metals; engineering grades are exceptions.
  • Viscoelastic behaviour, creep and relatively high thermal expansion can affect dimensions under load/heat.
  • Thermoplastics can often be remelt-processed; thermosets cure and do not simply remelt. Additives/reinforcement change properties.

No single property applies identically to every plastic.

Q5(b)Select a suitable method to manufacture disposable PET water bottles and explain it.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Selected method: injection stretch blow moulding (ISBM), usually a two-stage route for PET bottles.

  1. Injection mould a thick-walled PET preform with the finished neck/thread; the preform is not yet the bottle.
  2. Condition/reheat the preform to the specified stretch-blow window and locate it in a bottle mould.
  3. A stretch rod elongates it axially while high-pressure air expands it radially against the cooled cavity (biaxial stretching).
  4. Hold/cool, open the mould and eject the bottle; inspect neck, wall distribution, clarity, dimensions and leakage.

Stretching improves strength/barrier performance for lightweight bottles. PET preform injection plus stretch-blow is distinct from ordinary extrusion blow moulding used for many other hollow containers.

Q5(c)Explain the working principle of arc welding with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Representative process: shielded metal arc welding (SMAW). Arc welding establishes an electric arc between an electrode and the work. The arc's heat melts the joint edges; in SMAW the flux-coated consumable electrode also melts to supply filler.

  1. A suitable AC/DC power source, insulated holder/electrode lead and work-return lead complete the welding circuit.
  2. The operator maintains the specified arc length and travel; the arc heats the electrode tip and base-metal edges to create a weld pool.
  3. Electrode metal transfers into the pool. Flux decomposes to provide shielding gas and forms slag that protects the solidifying bead.
  4. The arc moves along the prepared joint; the pool freezes behind it and joins the parts.
  5. After safe cooling, remove slag between passes when required and inspect bead/profile and specified acceptance criteria.

Arc length, current, electrode, material preparation and shielding must follow the qualified procedure. PPE, fume control, screens and electrical isolation are essential.

Q5(b)What is compression moulding? List the advantages of injection moulding over compression moulding.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Compression moulding: a measured charge (often thermoset) is placed in a heated matched mould; the press closes and heat/pressure make the material flow and cure or solidify before ejection.

Injection moulding advantages for suitable products:

  • Shorter, readily automated cycles and high output for repeat production.
  • Better ability to form intricate details, ribs, bosses and small features with a well-designed mould.
  • Consistent shot metering and good dimensional repeatability when material, mould and process are controlled.
  • Less manual charge placement and generally less flash/trim waste in a well-designed process.

Injection requires more complex/high-cost equipment and moulds and is not universally superior; compression is often suitable for large or thermoset parts.

Q1(a)State the types of core. Describe any one with a neat sketch.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

By position/support: horizontal, vertical, balanced, hanging/suspended, cover and drop cores. These describe how the core is located in the mould; dry-sand, shell and chemically bonded describe core-making material/process, a different classification basis.

Horizontal core: it lies horizontally in the mould cavity and is supported/located by core prints at its ends (or designed supports where necessary). The prints provide seats and help resist core movement from metal buoyancy. A core must also have sufficient strength, gas permeability and collapsibility. Inspect location before closing the mould; a shifted core gives incorrect wall thickness or a blocked passage.

Q1(b)Define pattern and state the types of pattern. Describe any one briefly.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

A pattern is a tool used to form the mould cavity corresponding to the casting, with required allowances, parting features and core prints. It is not the finished casting itself.

Types: single-piece, split/two-piece, multi-piece, loose-piece, match-plate, cope-and-drag, gated, sweep, skeleton, segmental and follow-board patterns.

Match-plate pattern: cope and drag halves are mounted on opposite sides of a common plate, often with gates attached. The plate gives repeatable alignment and suits machine/high-volume moulding, but tooling is more complex and costly than a simple single-piece pattern.

Q1(c)Classify special casting processes and explain the die-casting process with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Examples/classification of special casting: permanent-mould casting (gravity and pressure die casting, hot- or cold-chamber); centrifugal casting (true, semi-centrifugal and centrifuging); precision/expendable-pattern routes (investment/lost-wax and lost-foam); and other specialised routes such as shell, squeeze, low-pressure and continuous casting. Lists vary by text; these names describe process principles rather than one exclusive universal taxonomy.

Pressure die casting — cold-chamber cycle assumed for the sketch:

  1. Close and clamp the reusable metal die halves; cavity, cores/slides, vents and runner/gate are aligned.
  2. For a compatible alloy such as aluminium, ladle a measured charge from a separate melting furnace into the shot sleeve.
  3. A plunger drives the liquid through the runner and gate into the die cavity at controlled high speed/pressure while displaced air exits through vents or a designed vacuum system.
  4. Hold pressure while the thin casting solidifies against the cooled die.
  5. Open the die, eject the casting and attached runner/overflow, trim and inspect; prepare the die for the next guarded cycle.

Die casting offers high production rate, detail and finish, but needs costly dies/clamping equipment and compatible alloys. Hot-chamber machines immerse injection hardware in the bath and are used only with compatible lower-melting alloys.

Q1(b)Define pattern and state the types of pattern. Describe any one briefly.4 marks⌄

A pattern is a tool used to form the mould cavity corresponding to the casting, with required allowances, parting features and core prints. It is not the finished casting itself.

Types: single-piece, split/two-piece, multi-piece, loose-piece, match-plate, cope-and-drag, gated, sweep, skeleton, segmental and follow-board patterns.

Match-plate pattern: cope and drag halves are mounted on opposite sides of a common plate, often with gates attached. The plate gives repeatable alignment and suits machine/high-volume moulding, but tooling is more complex and costly than a simple single-piece pattern.

Q2(a)Classify the metal-joining processes.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Mechanical fastening: detachable bolts/screws or permanent rivets and related mechanical joints.
  • Welding: fusion methods (arc, gas, resistance) and solid-state methods (e.g. friction/ultrasonic), selected by material and joint.
  • Brazing and soldering: a lower-melting filler wets/join surfaces while base metal remains solid; conventional filler-liquidus boundary is 450°C.
  • Adhesive bonding: a suitable polymer adhesive transfers load across prepared surfaces.

Classifications can overlap in industrial standards. Select by material compatibility, thickness, load, access, heat distortion, production and inspection needs.

Q2(b)Explain thermit welding in metal-joining process.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Thermit welding is a specialist fusion process in which an approved aluminothermic reaction produces intense heat and molten weld metal. A common simplified reaction is Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat.

  1. Trained staff align and restrain the parts and fit a dry refractory mould around the joint.
  2. Preheat/prepare only as the qualified proprietary procedure specifies.
  3. The approved reaction charge is initiated by authorised personnel within a protected exclusion area; no charge ratio or ignition instructions are included.
  4. Molten metal flows through the designed delivery path into the joint cavity; slag is separated by the mould/crucible arrangement.
  5. After specified solidification/cooling, remove the mould, trim/finish and inspect the joint.

It is useful for field rail and selected heavy-section joints, but requires strict controls for extreme heat, molten metal and reaction products.

Q2(c)State the working of resistance welding and explain spot welding with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Resistance-welding principle: low-voltage, high current passes through contacting workpieces while electrodes apply force. Electrical resistance at the interface generates heat approximately Q = I²Rt; the required time, current and force produce a local weld without a separate filler electrode.

Spot-welding equipment: transformer/power source and timer/controller, opposed copper-alloy electrodes, force actuator, water-cooled holders, overlap fixture and safety guarding.

  1. Clean and overlap the sheets; align the opposed electrodes at the required spot.
  2. Apply squeeze force before current so contact is established.
  3. Pass a controlled high-current pulse; interface heat forms a molten nugget between the sheets.
  4. Stop current but maintain force during hold so the nugget solidifies under restraint.
  5. Release electrodes and index the work for the next spot; inspect indentation and specified weld tests.

Used for sheet assemblies such as vehicle bodies. It needs access from both sides, and a good-looking surface alone does not prove nugget size or joint strength.

Q2(a)State different safety precautions in metal-joining processes.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

  • Use a correctly shaded welding helmet, safety glasses, flame-resistant clothing, gloves and safety footwear; screen nearby people from arc UV/IR.
  • Provide local fume extraction/ventilation and use suitable respiratory protection when the risk assessment requires it.
  • Inspect rated, insulated leads/holders; connect the work return correctly, keep equipment dry and isolate before adjustment/maintenance.
  • Secure gas cylinders upright; use gas-specific regulators/hoses and required flashback/non-return protection. Keep oxygen equipment free of oil/grease.
  • Remove combustibles, follow hot-work permit/fire-watch rules and never weld sealed or unknown containers.

Q2(b)Write a short note on the various gas flames used in gas welding.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Neutral: nearly balanced oxygen/acetylene; distinct inner cone plus outer envelope, without an acetylene feather. It is the general-purpose choice for many alloys.

Carburising/reducing: excess acetylene produces an inner cone, visible acetylene feather and outer envelope; it may add carbon and is used only when compatible.

Oxidising: excess oxygen gives a short, sharp inner cone with outer envelope; it can oxidise the work and is suited only to specified materials/processes.

The cone/feather/envelope shapes and relative lengths are shown schematically. Use the approved qualified procedure to select and set a flame.

Q2(c)List various types of arc-welding processes and explain any one with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Types: shielded metal arc (SMAW), MIG/MAG or gas-metal arc (GMAW), TIG/GTAW, submerged arc (SAW), flux-cored arc (FCAW), plasma arc and carbon-arc welding.

SMAW working: a power source sends current through an insulated holder and flux-coated consumable electrode to the work return. An arc between electrode tip and work melts the joint edges and electrode core. Droplets transfer into the pool; coating produces shielding gas and slag. The welder advances the electrode along the prepared joint while maintaining prescribed arc length/travel. The pool solidifies behind the arc, slag is removed safely between passes, and the weld is inspected after cooling.

Equipment includes AC/DC source, leads/holder, electrode, work clamp, joint fixtures, screen, fume extraction and PPE. It is portable and versatile, but electrode changes, slag removal, skill and fume control limit productivity.

Q3(a)Define metal-forming process and give a detailed classification.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Metal forming shapes solid metal by controlled plastic deformation under forces, without removing chips or filling a molten-metal mould.

  • Bulk deformation: rolling, forging/upsetting, extrusion and wire/bar/tube drawing.
  • Sheet-metal forming: bending, stretch forming and deep drawing (cutting/blanking operations are related sheet processes but are not themselves plastic forming).
  • By temperature: hot, warm or cold working, relative to the material's recrystallisation behaviour.
  • By loading/tool route: compressive processes, tensile drawing, bending and combined forming; processes can also be classified by open/closed tooling or continuous/discrete production.
Q3(b)Define forging. Describe any one forging process with a neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Forging is shaping metal by compressive force from a hammer or press, usually between dies. It may be hot, warm or cold according to material/process.

Open-die upsetting example:

  1. Place a suitable billet between relatively simple upper and lower dies.
  2. Apply controlled compressive blows/load, reducing height and increasing cross-section; reposition/rotate as required to obtain the desired section.
  3. Use measured stock, temperature and die support; finish with required machining allowance.
  4. Inspect size, surface folds/cracks and specified integrity.

Open-die forging suits large/simple or low-volume parts and flexible shapes, but depends on operator skill and has lower dimensional precision than closed-die production.

Q3(c)State different methods of extrusion process. Explain any one with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Methods: direct/forward extrusion, indirect/backward extrusion, hydrostatic extrusion and impact extrusion. Processes can also be hot or cold; hollow products use mandrels/bridge dies as designed.

Direct (forward) extrusion: a ram pushes a billet through a stationary die; metal exits in the same direction as ram motion.

  1. Place a prepared billet in the container and align the die at its outlet.
  2. A dummy block transmits the press force; the container confines the billet laterally.
  3. The ram advances and compresses the billet; when stress exceeds flow resistance, metal passes through the die opening.
  4. Continue the controlled stroke to produce a continuous section; stop before the specified butt/discard region.
  5. Cut, cool/straighten as required and inspect dimensions and surface.

It makes bars, rods, channels and constant-section profiles. Tool loads, container friction, butt loss and die wear are limitations. Indirect extrusion reduces billet/container sliding; hydrostatic uses pressurised fluid; impact uses a rapid stroke on a slug.

Q3(a)Define recrystallisation temperature and explain it.3 marks⌄

Model answer · 3 marks

Recrystallisation temperature is the temperature range at which new, relatively strain-free grains form and replace the deformed, work-hardened grains during a specified time.

During heating after cold work, recovery first relieves some stored strain; recrystallisation nucleates/grows new grains and restores ductility; further heating/time can cause grain growth. The temperature is not a single universal material constant: it depends on alloy, prior cold reduction, purity, heating time and initial grain condition. Hot working is defined relative to this behaviour.

Q3(c)State methods of drawing operation and explain any one with a neat sketch.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Drawing methods: wire drawing; bar/rod drawing; tube drawing by sinking (no internal tool), plug drawing or mandrel drawing; and sheet-metal deep drawing (cup drawing). Wire/bar/tube drawing pulls stock through a reducing die; deep drawing uses a punch and die to form a sheet blank into a cup.

Wire drawing:

  1. Clean and lubricate a ductile rod/wire and select a planned reduction/pass schedule.
  2. Point the leading end and pass it through a converging die; secure it in a drawbench or capstan.
  3. Pull under tension so the die reduces area and length increases; the bearing controls final size.
  4. Repeat passes/anneal only as specified; measure diameter and inspect surface, straightness and cracks.

A die, holder, pulling drive, lubricant, stock preparation and guarding are required. It produces accurate wire with good finish, but excessive reduction, friction or low ductility can cause cracking.

Q4(a)List various plastic-moulding processes with applications.3 marks⌄

Q4(b)Explain the plastic-extrusion moulding process with a figure.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Plastic extrusion continuously plasticises polymer in a heated barrel and forces the melt through a shaped die.

  1. Prepared resin feeds from a hopper into the rotating screw/barrel.
  2. Screw conveying, shear and controlled heat soften/meter the polymer into a uniform melt.
  3. The screw pushes melt through the die; the opening sets the continuous profile (pipe, sheet, film, rod or section).
  4. Downstream sizing/calibration and cooling fix dimensions; haul-off controls line speed, then the product is cut or wound.

It is efficient for long constant-cross-section products. Die swell, cooling distortion, pressure and material-conditioning limits must be controlled.

Q4(c)State types of furnaces used in metal casting. Draw and label a cupola furnace.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Furnaces used for casting melts: cupola (predominantly cast iron); crucible (fuel-fired or electrically heated, often non-ferrous); pit furnace (below-floor arrangement); electric resistance; coreless/channel induction; electric arc; and reverberatory/rotary furnaces for suitable alloys. These names do not form one exclusive classification: “crucible” describes the metal-holding vessel, “pit” its installation and “electric” the heat source.

Cupola labels and function: vertical steel shell and refractory lining; stack/exhaust; charging door for alternate metal/coke/flux charge; coke bed; wind belt and tuyeres admitting air at combustion level; slag hole above the metal tap; well/hearth collecting molten iron; and bottom/drop doors. Its zones are the preheating/stack, melting, reducing, combustion and well regions. Rising gases preheat descending charge; coke combustion supplies heat; metal melts and collects in the hearth while slag floats above it.

Cupolas are predominantly used for cast iron. Furnace selection depends on alloy, melt temperature, contamination, batch/continuous output, energy and safe handling.

Q4(b)Explain blow-moulding process with a neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Extrusion-blow moulding (representative route):

  1. An extruder produces a hot hollow parison and places it between open halves of a split mould.
  2. The mould closes and pinches the parison; a blow pin/air route seals and supplies controlled air.
  3. Air pressure expands the soft tube against the cooled cavity, forming the container's outer shape.
  4. After the wall cools sufficiently, vent air, open the mould, eject and trim flash; inspect wall distribution and leakage.

It suits bottles, containers and hollow tanks. Parison control, neck/flash design and wall-thickness uniformity are important. PET bottles generally use injection stretch-blow rather than this basic extrusion-parison route.

Q4(c)Draw the gating system and state its types with the function of each component.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

A gating system delivers liquid metal into a mould cavity while controlling flow, erosion, aspiration, gas and inclusions.

  • Pouring basin/cup: receives the stream and steadies entry.
  • Tapered sprue: conducts metal downward; taper helps keep the accelerating stream full and limit aspiration.
  • Sprue well: reduces impact/turbulence at the direction change.
  • Runner: distributes metal from the sprue toward ingates.
  • Runner extension/filter: can trap some slag/inclusions in a properly designed system.
  • Ingate: controls the entry location and directs metal into the cavity.
  • Vent: allows displaced gas to escape; it is not the main metal-flow passage.

Types: top gating (short path but may splash/erode); bottom gating (quieter upward fill but longer path/heat loss); parting-line gating (convenient for split moulds). Gating ratios classify sprue:total runner:total ingate area; pressurised/unpressurised layouts are designed for alloy and casting, not universal ratios. A riser feeds solidification shrinkage and is distinct from the gating passages.

Q5(b)List various types of rolling mills and explain a four-high rolling mill with neat sketch.4 marks⌄

Model answer · 4 marks

Types: two-high (reversing/non-reversing), three-high, four-high, cluster/multi-high, tandem/continuous, universal and planetary mills.

Four-high mill: two small work rolls act on the strip; two large backup rolls support them and reduce roll bending. Strip enters the roll bite, thickness is reduced by the set gap and length increases. The rolls are carried in bearings/chocks in a rigid stand and driven in opposite directions. Backup support permits thin sheet/strip rolling with good thickness control, though alignment, roll crown and force still affect flatness.

Q5(c)List equipment used for gas welding and explain their working.7 marks⌄

Model answer · 7 marks

Equipment: oxygen and fuel-gas cylinders/manifold; gas-specific regulators and gauges; separate compatible hoses; required non-return/flashback protection; welding torch with mixer, valves and tip/nozzle; filler rod and flux where required; fixtures; PPE, screens and fume extraction.

Working principle: controlled oxygen and acetylene meet in the torch and burn at the tip. The flame heats the prepared joint edges to a molten condition; compatible filler is added when required, and the pool solidifies into the joint. The torch/tip, gas mixture, travel and filler must suit the base alloy and qualified procedure.

  1. Trained personnel inspect approved, rated equipment and the work area.
  2. Secure cylinders and confirm gas-specific lines, regulators, protective devices, joint preparation and ventilation.
  3. Use the authorised procedure to establish the specified flame and bring edges to the required molten state.
  4. Add filler/flux as specified while moving along the joint to obtain fusion.
  5. Shut down, cool, make safe and inspect only by the site procedure.

Oxy-acetylene is portable and useful for thin sections/repair, but has a broad heat-affected zone and slower heating than many arc methods. No cylinder-lighting sequence or pressure setting is prescribed here.
